Below the Abbey: update 3.562 (changes since 3.534, the update on itch.io before this one, including the last Hollow Grasslands quests and the way down to the Deeper Hollow)

The Hollow Grasslands: the last quests, and the way down to the Deeper Hollow

* Once the party has found all four books and the grassland language can be understood, Aldous Wend, the wizard in Exsce, has something more to tell it. Ask him whether something is wrong in the grassland. Since the Hollow Child-King fell, he says, the corruption has not stopped. It runs from the Orange Spire down into the ground and comes up again in the wax of the hives, in the pond water, and in the way the grubs have started to look at people. It is rising from a place further down, the deeper Hollow Lands, and nobody he knows has seen them.
* He tells the party the way down is an old gate that has not been opened since the first king, and that he does not know where it is. The great flying creatures that drift over the cavern can see all of it from above: Old Lilac, the great moth, in Glorm, and Bulb, the great whale, over Nibb. This starts a chain of three main quests, shown with the other main quests, and each one starts by itself when the last is handed in.
* The Corruption in the Deep (first quest): find Old Lilac in Glorm. She hangs from the roof of the plaza, silent, until the party comes with the wizard's errand. Speaking to her hands the quest in. Reward: 200 gold and 900 experience. Aldous reminds the party where to find her if it asks again ("Be polite to her. She is very old, and she is not well.").
* Jelly for the Moth (second quest): Old Lilac is weak, for the corruption is in the air she breathes, and she cannot fly so far. She asks for 6 jars of royal jelly from the hives, taken from the bag when they are handed in. Reward: 400 gold, 1,400 experience and a potion. In return she tells the party that the gate lies under the bell tower of Nibb, in the north, sealed since the first king, and that Bulb can find it from above and is strong enough to carry the party down.
* The Whale's Errand (third quest): Bulb, a great pale whale on a bulb of warm light, will not speak until the party comes from Old Lilac. The hive creatures round the gate are too thick for her to fly through, so the party must kill 8 creatures of the hives (any grubs, wasps, golems, swarms or bosses of the hives count, and the count starts from when the quest is taken). Reward: 600 gold, 2,200 experience and two potions.
* When the third quest is handed in, Bulb gives the party a coil of silver thread: a magic map. From then on the map of the maze draws a thin, glowing, dashed line of everywhere the party has walked. The party is told of it the first time it enters the maze.
* Ask Bulb to carry the party down ("Climb on, little ones. Hold the fin and do not look down.") and she lifts it higher than the towers and flies north to the old gate under the bell tower of Nibb, which swings open on its own. The party arrives at the bottom of the Deeper Hollow, at the door in the south of the maze, and the message reads: "The air is thick and sweet here, and the walls are black with something that moves." The party can go back up through the same door at any time.
* What waits below is no longer a short stretch of cavern that ends at a plaque. The Deeper Hollow is now a huge maze of two hundred by two hundred squares, with eight towns, danger zones, secret doors, coloured keys and gem doors, a quest for every town, the Underlevel under it, and (since this update) thieves, burning spheres and shops. The Hollowed Warden sits in the great atrium at the top of the maze, in the Black Hall, and the last of the twelve town quests is to kill it ("The Warden in the Dark"), so the maze can breathe. A plaque beside the Black Hall still marks the end of the road for now.

The thieves of the maze

* Four thieves wander the Deeper Hollow, one in each quarter of the maze. They keep out of the towns and the danger zones. Each shows on the mini map as a red mark with a dark hood, so he can be seen and avoided.
* When the party is near and carrying gold, a thief creeps up behind it along the passages, one square at a time, taking the shortest way through the corridors. He only starts creeping when the party is within about fourteen squares.
* If he reaches the square behind the party, the party gets about a second and a half to turn round. Face him in time and he is caught: he draws a knife and a real fight begins. Fail to face him and he takes up to 500 gold from the party's purse, then runs off with it. After running he is out of sight for a good while and then goes home.
* A thief in front of you does not creep. If the party turns to face one that is within three squares in a clear line, the fight starts at once.
* Kill a thief that is carrying the party's gold and the gold falls and goes straight back to the party ("The thief drops 500 gold. It is yours again.").
* A Thief, Ranger or Assassin in the party hears a thief coming and says so, once, when he is about eight squares away: "A thief is near, be on guard."
* Each thief lives in a lone house in a clearing of his own quarter. Four houses in all, each a different picture, clear of the towns and the danger zones. Whenever the party comes near a house, its thief is at home, standing at the door. When the party goes away he wanders again.
* Knock on a thief's door and talk, and he tries to talk the party out of accusing him: three different excuses for each of the four thieves. Keep to the accusation all three times and he gives back every coin he stole from the party. Waver ("Maybe I was wrong") and he sends the party away, and they can try again. A thief with nothing of yours just chats, and treats an accusation as a rumour. Or draw a knife on him: he grabs a knife and the fight is on.
* If the party has killed a thief, his house is empty and a chest stands by the door, holding some of his own gold plus anything of the party's he still had.
* The people of the maze mention the thieves in their gossip.

The burning spheres and the Sunfire Blade

* Six huge burning spheres roam the Deeper Hollow, painted pictures of balls of roaring fire: molten orange, white-hot yellow, crimson, blue, green and one with a dark cracked core. They never enter a town, and they stay within about forty-five squares of where they began.
* They show on the mini map from far off as big orange rings, so they can be seen coming and avoided.
* A sphere that notices the party (within five squares, in sight) chases it, but it misses a step one round in three, so it is slower than the party walks. If the party gets about nine squares away it loses them and goes back to wandering. Close enough to touch, it attacks.
* Cinder, the ember warden of Gildmere, asks the party to kill all six. The quest counts spheres killed before it was taken. The reward is the Sunfire Blade, 12,000 gold and 60,000 experience.
* The Sunfire Blade is a level 65 sword: 234 to 342 damage, bosses take 30% more from it, and it costs 8,000 gold.
* Each sphere drops a level 70 weapon the first time it is killed. See below.

Weapons

* A new level 70 tier of six weapons, one from each sphere, each with a long description and stronger effects than anything before: Molten Greatblade (sword: a foe under 40% health takes four times the blow, bosses take 60% more, wounds bleed), Whitefire Warhammer (mace: four blows in ten stun the foe, the ground shudders), Crimson Cleaver (axe: each blow carries on to the foe behind, half the hits leave a bleeding wound), Bluefire Longbow (bow: shoots seven squares, arrows pass through, half the time a second arrow follows), Verdant Flame Staff (adds 90 to spell power, bosses take 60% more, foes reel dazed) and Cinder Dagger (a foe under half health takes four times the blow, most wounds bleed). Each needs level 70 and costs 10,000 gold.
* Their damage is 265 to 370 (staff 125 to 175).
* The weapon curve was reworked. The four level 28 boss weapons (Hollow Scepter, Veilpiercer, Crownbreaker, The Duke's Gavel) keep their full damage and now cost 2,500 gold each. Every ordinary weapon from level 30 to 64 hits harder than them: about 3.4 times the old damage at level 30, easing down to 2.6 times at level 50, 2.9 times for the odd ones above. Then 10% was taken off the damage of every weapon of level 30 and above.

The Underlevel

* The Underlevel is far darker. The walls are old black-brown brickwork covered in claw scratches and dried blood, in two different brick patterns. The floor is near-black stone. The air is close and almost no light carries: torches and lanterns now help only a little,.
* Twenty new painted creatures live there, replacing the old insects and grubs. Ten black slime monsters (a tall dripping humanoid, a puddle of many eyes, a tentacled ooze, a slime spider, a slime serpent, a screaming-maw blob, a slime hound, a slime holding a screaming skull, a many-armed horror and a great mass with dozens of eyes) and ten demonic horrors (a maggot demon with many mouths, a headless crawler with eyes in its chest, a skinned hound, a centipede with a human face, a split-jawed ghoul, a horned spider demon, a tentacled eye horror, a leather-winged imp, a pile of fused skulls and a spined bone beast). Every one has red glowing eyes. The bodies are darkened to near black and the eyes glow, so in the dark most of what you see is red eyes.
* Ten demon-insect bosses wander the corridors, far apart from each other: a mantis, scorpion, beetle, wasp, centipede, spider, moth, locust, fly and cicada demon, painted as a cross between a demon and an insect. They show on the mini map as orange rings and move like the spheres: they chase slower than the party can walk and lose it if it gets away.
* The ladders between the maze and the Underlevel are now a single ladder with plain stone above it, instead of two ladders stacked one on top of the other.
* The bites of the new creatures leave poison, bleeding, fever and stings, plus the old shaken and grave rot.

Shops in the maze

* Every kind of shop the rest of the game has now works in each of the eight maze towns: a weaponsmith, an armourer, a magic seller, a general trader and an inn, forty more people in all, on top of the shops that were already there. Each is a named person from that town (such as Brand, the weaponsmith of Hedgefoot).
* The weaponsmith sells the basic weapons and a rotating selection of better ones from level 15 to level 50. The armourer sells the basic armour, two full sets of the iron, ember or void armour (a different pair in each town) and a few better single pieces. The magic seller sells scrolls, rings, staves and draughts of mana. The trader sells torches, lanterns, food, herbs, potions and lockpicks. The inn rents a bed for 60 gold: it heals the party and feeds it. It also sells food.

The maps

* The Deeper Hollow map is redrawn as a hand-inked medieval map, painted at five times the old sharpness. Soft watercolour washes colour each biome and run into each other, passages are inked in wobbly rounded pen lines instead of a grid of squares, the walls are hatched with short pen strokes, clearings have tufts of grass, flowers and pebbles, atriums have ferns, rooms have scattered flagstones and ponds have ripples and lily pads.
* Trees on the map are now painted: an oak, a yew and a bell-shaped tree with golden blossoms, and a fountain, instead of green dots. Painted pictures also mark the thieves' houses, the dock and steps of the water passages and the huts of the towns.
* The thieves' houses are on the map, each with its name.
* The water passages are on the map. Shallow water that can be walked through is light blue, and deep water that needs a boat (or a helm, in the Drowned Maze) is dark blue. Reeds mark the docks of the canals, and a pond marks each flight of steps down into the Drowned Maze. The lakes of the maze are dark blue on the map with a dock icon at each dock, and the marshes light blue. A Drowned button shows the whole Drowned Maze in green-blue. The key lists both kinds of water.
* Standing on a woken teleport stone and opening the map, every other woken stone is ringed in blue on the whole map and on each quarter. Tap a ringed stone to travel to it.
* The Underlevel map is far more sinister: near-black and blood-red parchment, blood pools and spatters, claw scratches, crossed bones, skulls and watching eyes, a dripping red title and a bloody frame, with the ladders glowing so they can still be found.
* The key on the map lists the thieves' houses, canal docks, steps into the water, and shallow and deep water.

The mini map

* A plus and a minus button beside the mini map zoom it in and out. The minus can zoom out a long way (about four times).
* Burning spheres, demon bosses and thieves show on it in the maze and the Underlevel as described above.

Music

* The Underlevel plays Dark Cavern Ambient (CC0, Paul Wortmann).
* The Deeper Hollow maze and its boat canals play Paranoid Truth (CC0, Tsorthan Grove).
* The Hollow Land and the Hollow Grasslands play Searching (CC0, yd) when you are outdoors.

Areas and gates

* Go to area now lists the Hollow Grasslands, its four beehive dungeons (the Hush, Droning, Amber and Queen's Hives) and the Drowned Maze.
* The gate between the Hollow Land and the Hollow Grasslands now goes both ways. A spiked gate on the ledge of the Hollow Land leads down into the Grasslands, as the southern gate of the Grasslands leads back out onto the ledge.

The Drowned Maze, made fresh

* The Drowned Maze is now a freshwater dungeon, half its old size (43 by 43 cells), and greenish instead of blue. The walls are thick with moss and slime, hung with trailing weed, the floor is silty pond bottom, and there are reeds, lily pads and green weed everywhere to swim through. Freshwater fish of eight kinds (carp, perch, trout, goldfish, bream, pike, tench and shoals of minnows) swim by.
* Ten freshwater creatures live there, all painted: giant piranhas, marsh water snakes, water scorpions, diving-bell spiders, giant leeches, giant water beetles, mud crayfish, snapping turtles and bloodworms, and Old Whiskers, a huge whiskered catfish that is the warden of the maze and sleeps in the farthest chamber with the prize chest. Their bites leave bleeding, stings, poison and fever.
* A teleport stone stands in the farthest chamber, next to the warden, and another stands in the clearing beside the first steps down. Wake each by standing on it. From either one you can travel to the other, and the stone at the far end also takes you to any other woken stone of the maze.
* Four people live in the clearing beside the first steps and give nine quests that give a reason to go under the water: The Lost Urn and Reeds for the Wreaths (Anselm Dray, the sexton), Two Wedding Rings and Leeches for the Healer (Odile Pemm), The Moonscale Carp, Piranha Pie and Pests in the Pantry (Brannoc Gule, the cook), and The Diver's Journal and Old Whiskers (Wynne Tarn, the diver's daughter). Each person offers the next quest when the last is handed in. The urn, the two rings, the carp, the journal and the reed bundles are lying in the chambers of the maze.

The lakes and the boats

* The old canal levels (the Vine Canal and the Croc Canals) are gone. In their place the maze itself has three lakes: one in the south-west (15 by 11 cells), one in the east (15 by 15) and one in the north-centre (17 by 15). Each has ragged shores, reeds growing out of the water, lily pads, small islets of reeds (two of them with a chest), hanging vines on the walls, and crocodiles, eels and vine snakes in the water.
* Each lake has two docks, a departure dock and a far dock, in different parts of the maze, 84 to 136 steps apart on land. Walking along a corridor you come to a couple of steps going down into the water, and a boat. Step in and the view changes to the front of a wooden boat, rocking gently, with the water ahead. Steer it with the ordinary arrows. The boat only goes on water and the party cannot wade into deep water. Bump a dock from the boat to land.
* In the boat the sound changes to water: lapping and oar dips instead of footsteps, with a soft water hush, as on the ship in the Narrow Sea.
* Butterflies, bees and dragonflies flit over each lake and its reeds.
* Quests next to the departure docks: Reed, the canal warden, and Bram the boatwright (south-west lake): Crocodile Skins, Eels in the Lake, Snake Bounty, The Far Docks (land at the far dock of all three lakes), Reeds for the Roofs and The Lost Net. Fenn the beekeeper (north-centre lake): Lilies for the Bees. Odda the ferrywoman (east lake): A Letter for the Hermit, to Old Marl, who lives beside that lake's far dock.

The Flooded Quarter

* A big part of the south east of the maze is now deep water: the Flooded Quarter, a real maze about 59 by 55 squares in which the passages themselves are deep water between high walls, with a few wider basins of lily pads. It can only be crossed by boat. Four docks lead into it, on its west, north, south and east sides, each at the end of a land corridor with a wide wooden landing. A boat can go from any dock to any other.
* It holds 34 crocodiles, eels and vine snakes (in groups, never next to a dock), reed beds, lily pads, hanging vines, heron egg clutches on the islets, treasure on islets in the basins, and butterflies, bees and dragonflies. The map shows it dark blue with its name.
* Tamsin, the channel pilot, stands at the west dock and gives three quests: Chart the Flooded Quarter (disembark at all four docks), Heron Eggs (bring 5 clutches from the islets) and Clear the Channels (kill 12 creatures of the water maze).
* Five chests holding exactly 1,000 gold each are on islets in the lakes and the Flooded Quarter, guarded by a crocodile and an eel, and the other chests on the islets now always give a real reward (300 to 900 gold plus potions and sometimes armour). Some lake chests were empty before.

Boats: embark and disembark

* Boats on the lakes and the Flooded Quarter now work like the ship of the Narrow Sea. Bumping a dock no longer asks anything. The boat lies at anchor at the dock where it was left, drawn as a rowing boat, and on land within eight squares of it an Embark button appears. In the boat, within three squares of a dock landing, a Disembark button appears. Landing at a far dock still counts for the far dock quests.
* The three lakes are much bigger (about 25 to 27 squares wide and 19 to 23 high, nearly three times the area), with more creatures, islets and butterflies and bees in each.
* The reeds in the lakes are much lower, and the bigger reeds on the islets are lower still, so you can see across the water.
* The wall block at each dock, which showed the picture of the boat twice, one above the other, now shows one dock picture with plain stone above it.

The black sea of the Underlevel

* A huge black sea fills about a fifth of the Underlevel (about 8,000 squares of deep black water in one body), with a ragged coast of bays and headlands, rock pillars, reefs and islets. Rivers wind out of the sea and run beside the labyrinth's passageways, within a square or two of the corridors with a rock bank between, before ending in dead ends. Nothing crosses the water: there are no bridges or walkways over the sea or the rivers, and the passageways of the labyrinth run round them, so the whole labyrinth can still be walked. The water is almost black, the wind howls and the surf is loud near the water (full volume in a boat, fading to nothing beyond twelve squares).
* There are 11 boats at anchor, one at each beach landing where a corridor meets a river, and one at a dock on the sea near the first ladder. Any boat can sail the whole water. The Narrow Sea Embark and Disembark buttons work: in the boat near a shore, a Disembark button puts the party ashore (not with an enemy next to it); on land within eight squares of a boat, an Embark button puts the party aboard.
* 160 horrors swim in the black water and attack the boat, ten painted kinds, all with red glowing eyes and near-black bodies: a leviathan with rows of teeth, a giant angler with a glowing red lure, a many-tentacled kraken horror, a drowned corpse swimmer, a giant black eel, a sea hag, a blind white abyssal crab, a swarm of bone fish, a black slime serpent and a hooked-jaw giant leech. The Black Leviathan guards the island in the middle of the sea.
* A treasure chest on the island in the middle of the sea holds 5,000 gold. Five more chests hold 1,000 gold each, on river banks and islets round the level, each guarded by two or three of the Underlevel's creatures.
* Around the Deeper Hollow, people whisper about a huge treasure below: that under the maze, past the ladders, lies a black sea with an island in the middle, with a chest on it worth more gold than a king has.
* The Underlevel map shows the sea and rivers as black water.
* Many more red eyes stare from the dark of the Underlevel: about 290 pairs and clusters of glowing red eyes hang at the far ends of straight corridors and at dead ends, blinking now and then, visible from far away. They are only a stare.

Lake reeds

* There are now many thin, sparse, see-through painted reeds in the lakes and the Flooded Quarter, so you can see across the water, with the dense clumps only now and then.

Little details everywhere in the maze

* The whole Deeper Hollow maze is dressed with about 2,900 little painted details. Nearly every passageway of three or more squares (89 in every hundred; the rest are right beside doors, docks, ladders and people) has something small and different in it, and no two neighbouring stretches have the same thing: a broken clay pot, a heap of bones and a skull, a candle burning in its wax, an open book, a cluster of glowing blue mushrooms, a fern in a crack, a cracked bucket, a rusty lantern, a pile of shells, a branch of coral, scattered coins, broken mosaic tiles, a gnarled root, an antler, a dripping piece of honeycomb, a bundle of reeds. Dead ends get something special. You walk straight through the small ones.
* Each of the 24 named areas of the maze has its own signature: the hedge areas have roots, ferns, mushrooms, bones and antlers; the stone warrens have cairns, standing stones, mason statues and rubble; the honeycomb areas have honeycomb, candle shrines and rings of pots; the shell galleries have rings of shells and coral; the sandstone deeps have scarab statues, hoards of coins and bones; the blue quarter has lantern shrines, crystal outcrops and rings of books; the mosaic halls have rings and spirals of broken tiles; and the gilded quarter has hoards of coins, lantern and book shrines and weeping child statues. Each area also has a trail of its main thing along its longest passage, and some a toppled pillar or a lone statue.
* 120 painted statues stand in the clearings, rooms and atriums and in dead-end niches of the maze: a hedge knight, a mason with a hammer, a bee queen, a shell maiden with a conch, a scarab beetle, a lapis owl, a hooded monk and a weeping child with a lantern. They block the way where they stand, but only in wide places, never in a one-square passage.
* About 600 rocks and stones: mossy boulders in the wide places, leaning slabs, small cairns, piles of pebbles, tall standing stones carved with spirals, broken pillars, heaps of rubble and crystal outcrops. Six big clearings have a ring of standing stones.
* 400 little pictures are hung on the walls of the corridors (the wall symbols and trailing vines), at least three squares apart.
* None of it is in water, on a dock or beside a door, a ladder, a teleport stone or a person, and the maze can still be walked everywhere it could before.

The marshes

* Eight stretches of the maze are marshes under warm, shallow water that can be waded through, thick with reeds, lilies and tall grass that the party walks straight through. The biggest is the Marsh Maze, and the others are named too (the Reed Flats, Lily Shallows, the Wading Rows, Heron Marsh, the Sedge Fens, Mudwater Court and the Rush Beds). The names are on the map and the water shows light blue. The first time the party wades into each, a message says where it is.
* Footsteps splash while the party is in the shallows: a soft, hissy wash of water noise, not a thump.
* Eight painted marsh creatures live in them, more in the Marsh Maze: giant locusts, giant mosquitoes, giant horseflies, razor dragonflies, marsh hornets, bog crickets, bloated bog toads and mire beetles.

Controls

* Drag one finger anywhere on the 3D view to look round: up and down, and left and right. The old look slider on the right side of the screen is gone. A plain tap still works as a tap, and the two-finger drag still works.

Other

* The loading screen of the web version (GitHub Pages) now shows a percentage and the megabytes, for example "Loading the pictures: 40%, 33 of 203 MB", the way the itch.io version already did. The first visit downloads about 200 MB of pictures.
* The clouds drifting in the maze were drawn as moving blocks with a cloud painted on them. They are now soft see-through clouds.
* On the Deeper Hollow map, the All button was hidden under the map's own buttons, so after choosing a quarter or another view there was no way back to the whole maze. The view buttons now sit beside the map's own buttons, and every view can return to the whole map. Fit, and Minus when the map cannot zoom out any further, also take a close-up quarter back out to the whole map, and a map opened afresh always shows the whole map. The Under button now shows the Underlevel map even before the party has been down there (it was blank until the Underlevel had been visited).
* Discarding an item in the inventory now asks first: "Discard (item)? It will be gone for good." with Discard and Keep it buttons.
* The little pet creatures wandering the maze and the clouds drifting through it were not moving at all. They move now, slowly, and the clouds only ever drift over open floor, never through a wall.

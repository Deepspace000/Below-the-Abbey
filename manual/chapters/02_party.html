Chapter 2Your Party and Combat

Four heroes go down into the dark. This is how they fight, how they fall, and how they get up again.

Your Party

You never play a lone adventurer in Below the Abbey. You steer a party of four, and the party lives or dies together. Their faces stand in a row along the bottom of the screen. Under each face are four thin bars: health in red, stamina or mana in blue, experience in yellow and hunger in brown. The small badge in the corner of a face is the hero's level, and the health bar carries the numbers, such as 34/34.

The first two portraits are your front line and the last two are your back line. When an enemy chooses whom to strike, a hero in the front line is three times as likely to be picked as one in the back. Keep your frail spell casters and healers at the back, as the ready-made party does.

Tap a portrait to choose which hero acts (in the pack screen you may also press 1 to 4). A darkened face means the hero has already acted this round. A face crossed with a red cross means the hero has fallen.

The ready-made party: a warrior, a thief, a mage and a priest, ready to go.

Making your own

On a new game you choose Ready-made (the four above), Random party (look, then start) or Make my own. Making your own means, for each hero, choosing a face from seventy portraits, a name, one of twelve occupations, and then spreading 3 free stat points over Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence. The same screen asks how the world should level: see Party Level and the World.

The Twelve Occupations

Every hero has one occupation. It sets their starting health, whether they run on stamina or mana, their three stats, their basic chance to hit, and what they gain at each level. These are the real level 1 numbers, before any gear. Stamina and mana work the same way: both pay for powers and both come back at the same rate. The names only tell you what kind of effort the power costs. Warriors, Barbarians, Rangers, Archers, Thieves, Assassins, Monks and the Goat use stamina. Paladins, Mages, Sorcerers and Priests use mana.

Strength, Dexterity, Intelligence

Each hero has three stats. Every one of them has a plain job in the rules.

STRENGTH

Adds 1 to weapon damage for every 4 points above 8. Heavy weapons and armour demand it: the Rusty Sword and the Iron Helm want STR 8, the Hand Axe wants 10. The party's combined Strength also sets how much you can carry: 20 kg plus 1.6 kg for every point of combined Strength, and 36 pack slots plus half a slot per point. Go over the limit and the party is overloaded: it walks at half speed and every hero loses 10 points of hit chance.

DEXTERITY

Adds 0.6% to hit chance for every point above 8 (and takes it away below 8). Bows ask for it (the Short Bow wants DEX 8). It is the first stat of the Ranger, Archer, Thief, Assassin and Monk.

INTELLIGENCE

Adds 1 spell damage for every 3 points above 10, and adds 1 to every heal for each 3 points above 8. A staff wants INT 6.

HEALTH

When it reaches nothing, the hero falls. Rings and armour can add to it, and so can some powers.

ARMOUR

Armour does not soak up damage. Instead every point of armour takes 3% off an enemy's chance to hit that hero.

Levels and Experience

Heroes earn experience (XP) from every creature the party kills. Each living hero receives the full amount: it is not divided among the four. A hero who has fallen earns nothing, so keep everyone on their feet.

A creature is worth its base XP times its own level, but one far below the party is worth less: 6% less for every level the party average stands above it, never dropping below 30% of the full reward. The fat rewards are in the creatures that stand at or above your level.

The XP needed for the next level is 15 x L x (L + 1), where L is the hero's current level. The bar restarts at zero each level.

When a hero levels up, a gold banner lists exactly what rose. They gain the health and stamina or mana shown in the table above, +1 to their first stat every level and +1 to their second every second level. They are healed by one and a half times their health gain, and their mana or stamina is refilled. Any new power is announced on the banner too.

The Character Sheet

Open the pack screen with I and tap the face tab, or press C. The sheet lists the chosen hero's level, experience (with the XP still to go), health, stamina or mana, the three stats, armour, damage range, hit chance and the party's gold. The paper doll to the left shows what the hero wears and wields. The four tabs along the bottom are Items I, Character C, Spells B and Powers K. Tap another face to read another hero.

Powers and Abilities

Every occupation owns nine powers, and each one is waiting on a level. They come at levels 1, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 and 80. The first is the hero's special and is there from the start. Some of the others are passive: always on, nothing to press. The rest are active: they cost stamina or mana and are used on demand. The last, at level 80, is a passive capstone.

Press K in the pack screen for the Powers page. The left-hand list shows the whole party and what each hero has set. The middle list shows every power of the chosen hero, locked ones greyed with the level they open at. Tap one to read it. For an active power you may choose Set as special, which puts it on the special button beside Attack, or Use now.

The special button shows the power's name and cost. A hero without enough stamina or mana cannot use it. Every hero regains 1 point of stamina or mana at the end of each round, and a full rest refills the pool.

Scrolls: the Mage and Sorcerer

The Mage and the Sorcerer do not have a fixed first power. Their special is Spell: it casts whichever spell scroll is active in the Spells tab (B). The Sorcerer adds +2 to the damage. Spell scrolls go into the four spell slots, and only these two occupations can use them. The Mage begins with the Firebolt scroll.

How Combat Works

Combat is turn based. Nothing happens until you act, and nothing moves while you think. The game works in rounds, and a round has two halves: first your four heroes, then the enemies.

A round, step by step

  1. Choose a hero and give them an action. Press Space to Attack, X for the special power, P to drink the favourite potion, or tap the off-hand and second-weapon windows beside the special button to use what they hold.
  2. Each living hero acts once. When a hero has acted, their face darkens and the next hero who can still act is chosen. You may act in any order by tapping a face.
  3. As soon as the last hero has acted, every enemy takes its turn, one after another.
  4. Then the round ends: every hero recovers 1 stamina or mana, timed powers and buffs count down one round, and a new round begins.

Every step you take is a round too, worth one minute on the party clock, and enemies near you take their turn after it. Turning on the spot costs a minute but gives them no turn. Press Z or tap Wait to pass the whole round without acting: every hero skips, and the enemies move. The game has no separate guard stance and no flee button. To avoid a fight, back away and shut a door, or simply walk out of the creature's sight.

Reading the blows

Every blow leaves a mark you can read. A gold number pops up over a foe that is hit. A red number floats up from the portrait of a hero who is hurt, and the portrait is washed red for a moment before it fades. A green plus number marks a heal. The Attack and special buttons flash a little picture of the blow: a slash, a thud, an arrow, a burst of magic or a holy cross. And the message line at the foot of the view always spells it out, with names and numbers.

Who can be hit, and from where

Your heroes strike the creature straight ahead. A sword, axe, mace, dagger or fist reaches 1 tile. A bow reaches further: the Short Bow shoots up to 3 tiles, and the Nymph's Bow up to 5. A bow hits the first enemy in line within its reach, even one standing next to you. If nothing is in reach, the game says so and the turn is not spent.

Enemies, however, can attack from any side. Messages tell you when a blow comes from the side or from behind. Move to face the foe that matters, and mind your back in a corridor.

Hit chance, damage and critical hits

Hit chance. A hero's chance to hit is: the occupation's base (see the table), plus the weapon's own bonus (a Dagger +4%, a Hand Axe -5%, a bare fist -10%), plus 0.6% for each point of DEX above 8, plus rings and passives. It is never below 20% or above 97%. Against flying creatures such as bats, subtract 20 points. An overloaded party loses 10. You can read your number on the character sheet.

The special powers of most occupations never miss: Power Strike, Twin Stab and the like do not roll to hit at all.

Damage. A weapon has a damage range, such as 3 to 5 for the Rusty Sword. The game rolls inside the range, then adds the Strength bonus (1 per 4 STR above 8) and any ring bonus. A bare fist does 1 to 2 (the Monk 3 to 5 and the Goat 2 to 5). Special powers multiply the roll: Power Strike is double plus 2, the Barbarian's Rage Blow two and a half times.

Critical hits. Every weapon has a small chance of a critical hit, worked out from its price: 1% for the cheapest, rising to a ceiling of 30% for the finest, plus whatever the weapon, rings and passive powers add. A critical hit does 1.5 times damage, and the message line says (critical! x1.5). A priest's heal can crit too: about one heal in eight is a critical heal, 75% stronger.

Bows and spells

A hero with a bow in hand shoots instead of swinging, at any foe up to the bow's reach, with a small bonus to hit (the Short Bow +2%). The Ranger and Archer's powers are all about the bow: Aimed Shot never misses and reaches 3 tiles, Volley fires two arrows that never miss up to 4 tiles. Archers and rangers can therefore hurt a foe before it reaches the party.

A spell is cast the same way, from the special button: Firebolt and Frost Shard reach 3 tiles and, like a bow, hit the first enemy in line. A spell has no hit roll. If no foe is in its path, the cast is cancelled and nothing is spent.

Healing in a fight

The Priest's Heal (4 mana) restores 8 to 12 health to the most wounded living ally, plus 1 for each 3 points of INT above 8, plus a bonus that grows with the priest's level. Healing grows with the power: every heal restores a share of the patient's own health that rises with the level of the power, from about 37% for one patient at power level 10 to about 73% at level 50 and the whole of it at level 80, and from about 18% for a whole party at level 10 to 42% at level 50 and 60% at level 80. The amounts printed beside a power are the least it heals. Heal fails with a message if everyone is already healthy. Healing potions are an action too: the Potion button on the right drinks the potion shown on it (your favourite if you have chosen one, otherwise the best) on the chosen hero (the Healing Potion restores 30 health, the Strong one 90 from level 20, and bigger ones come later). Drinking uses the hero's turn.

The Enemy's Turn

Most creatures start unaware. Some patrol, some stand still. A creature notices the party if you come within about 6 tiles and it can see you or reach you quickly, or the moment it is hurt or missed. Once alert, it walks straight at you by the shortest way, one tile at a time.

Fast creatures (bats and wolves among them) get two moves in a turn. Bats flutter about at random about one move in three. Archers and bounty hunters shoot from 2 tiles away. A frozen or stunned creature loses its next turn. The Barbarian's Savage Roar, the Thief's Smoke Bomb and a Sorcerer's Frost Nova can do this to several at once.

When a creature reaches the tile next to you, it attacks. It chooses one living hero. A hero in the front line is three times as likely to be picked as one in the back.

The creature's chance to hit is its own accuracy, less 3% for each point of the hero's armour, less any dodge bonus, never under 10% or over 95%. A hit does damage inside its range and the hero's portrait washes red.

Read the foe before you swing

Whenever a creature stands in line ahead, up to 4 tiles away, its plate appears at the top of the view. It shows the creature's name and level, and a tall red bar at the left holds its health as a number. The small panel beneath compares the foe with your party: level for each, health for each, and a gauge that gives the fight a verdict of Trivial, Easy, Fair fight, Hard or Deadly, with a guess of how many rounds the party needs to win. A creature one level above the party is never rated worse than Hard; only a creature two levels above or more is ever Deadly.

Mercy

A beaten bandit may beg. When a bandit is down to about a fifth of his health, there is a fair chance he throws down his weapon. If you let him be, after three rounds he thanks you, hands over a little gold and runs, and may tell you a rumour. Strike a man who has surrendered and the game remembers it: your honour and luck fall, and word spreads in the nearest town.

Afflictions and Status Effects

Damp crypts, cold nights, wolves, snakes and bad pies all leave their mark. An affliction belongs to one hero (a few strike the whole party) and appears as a tiny picture in the corner of their face. Most take a few points off Strength, Dexterity or Intelligence until they are cured, and the stat is given back the moment they end. A stat cannot be lowered below 1. The same affliction is not put on a hero twice: a second dose only renews the first.

Poison, bleeding and a few others drain health every round for a set number of rounds. They can cripple, but they cannot kill: a hero's health never drops below 1 from an affliction. A few conditions are good ones and show with a green frame.

Open Menu and the Afflictions and States page to read about every one. It tells you how it feels, how you catch it and how it is cured. The list marks each affliction that is on a hero now, or that you have met before.

Warned by the Watch is a mark from the city guard for walking into houses; it fades in two days.

Resting and Camping

Press R or tap Camp. You cannot rest with enemies close by. The Camp page offers Rest the night, which takes 8 hours of game time, and a handful of other things to do by the fire.

A night's rest heals every hero to full health, refills stamina and mana, cures whatever a night cures (see the table), and raises any fallen hero with half their health. It also costs food: eight hours take about a third of a full hunger bar. If a hero is starving, they heal only to half, so eat first.

Sleeping out in the wild is not safe. Each night in camp away from the towns carries about a 14% chance that thieves take 5 to 13% of your gold, and about 8% that bandits raid the camp. A scout among you, a cat, a dog or a duck, or good luck, lowers the odds.

A bed at an inn costs a little gold (15 to 25 in the inns of the north). An inn night does everything a camp night does, tops every hero's hunger up to at least 70, and grants Well Rested and Well Fed. There is a one in eight chance of a hangover afterwards.

Friends and rivals

Under the camp list, the page shows how each pair of heroes feels about each other. Friends (bond of 5 or more) dodge a little more, and if a friend falls they fight on in a fury: +30% damage for 10 rounds. Rivals (bond of -5 or less) hit 3% harder to prove a point, but lose 1% of their aim.

Hunger and Food

Each hero has a hunger bar from 100 down to 0. It empties at a steady rate of 25 points every 6 hours, so a full bar lasts a whole day of game time. Walking, fighting and resting all pass the clock.

Below 25 the bar turns orange and the game says that the hero is hungry. At 0 the hero is starving: they lose 1 health every 20 minutes (never the last point), and a night's rest heals them only to half. Eating fixes all of it. Some powers slow the hunger: the Ranger's Woodcraft by 25%, the Monk's Inner Calm and the Goat's Tough Hide by 30%, the Goat's Scavenger by 50%.

Food also heals. Eat from the pack: choose the hero, the food and use it. When a hero is both unhurt and nearly full, the game tells you they are not hungry or hurt.

When a Hero Falls

When a hero's health reaches 0, they fall. Their face is dimmed and crossed with a red cross, and the message line says so. They cannot act, cannot be chosen by enemies and earn no experience. The rest of the party fights on. Falling is not final, and there are four ways back.

  • Rest. Any night, in camp or at an inn, raises every fallen hero with half their maximum health.
  • The Priest's Revive (a level 50 power, 12 mana) raises one fallen ally at 40% health.
  • Resurrection, the Priest's level 50 wizard spell, raises every fallen hero at 70% and heals the living by half.
  • Friends do not forget: a friend of the fallen fights on in a fury for 10 rounds.

If all four heroes fall, the game ends with The Party Has Fallen. The screen shows creatures slain, rounds fought, survivors, gold and the level of each hero. You may load your most recent save or return to the menu. Save before a boss.

Party Level and the World

Every creature has a level, and level drives everything in the table above. The game offers two ways to set the levels, chosen when you start and changeable at any time under Menu, Settings, Gameplay.

Traditional (the default)

Every place has its own level, and the levels climb from place to place as the story leads you on. The world map shows each place's level. If a place is too hard, come back later, stronger. The great creatures stand a few levels above their place. The first crypt climbs from level 1 at the start to 2 in the middle and 3 near the Rat King, in either mode.

Scaled

Creatures grow with your party, for a smoother and slightly easier game. If your party outgrows a creature by more than 5 levels, it is raised to between 2 and 5 levels below you, once. Great creatures always stand a fixed number of levels above the party.

A mixed crowd

In either mode creatures in one place are not all at one level. About two in five stand at the place's full level, about 35% stand 1 to 3 below, and a quarter stand 4 to 7 below, so each fight is a mix of tough and easy foes, and the weak ones die fast. Bosses and the great creatures are never lowered.

The party's own level

When the game needs a single number for your party, it takes the average level of the living heroes. That is the level the foe's plate compares against, the level that cuts XP for weak creatures, and the level the Scaled world follows. A fallen hero does not count.

Part One: The Bestiary

Every creature in the game has a picture, a temper and a few habits. This part of the book sets them down in the order you are likely to meet them: the rats of the crypt, the wolves of the wood, the dead of the desert, the horrors of the Hollow Land and the things that live in the sea. Each entry gives its place, its health and the size of its blows, and anything the game lets a careful party use against it.

Treat the numbers as a guide, not a promise. They are the base values, as the creature would be at level 1. A creature standing in a place of a higher level is stronger, as the box on the next page explains. Most of the creatures that look like people (bandits, guards, nomads, skeletons in armour and the like) always carry gold, so a fallen foe is worth a look.

Names printed in bold in the entries are the named foes and the great creatures. They are never made weaker than their place, and the greatest of them stand several levels above it.

How strong is a creature?

Take the base health and multiply it by 1 + 0.45 × (level − 1). Take the base blow and multiply it by 1 + 0.3 × (level − 1). A level 20 Black Rat therefore has 50 × 9.55 = 478 health. A level 1 rat has 50.

In the fixed world (the default) a creature stands at the level of the place it lives in, but not all of them do. About two in five are at the place's level, about a third are one to three levels lower, and about a quarter are four to seven lower, so every fight is a mix of tough ones and easy ones. Named foes and great creatures are never lowered. The great creatures stand above the place: the entry says by how many. The crypts below the Abbey are the exception, climbing from level 1 at the stair to level 3 by the Rat King.

The Gameplay page of the menu can change all this: see the Level Ladder at the back of this chapter.

Below the Abbey: the Hollow Land

Beyond the sealed door lie four floors of the Hollow Abbey and the Hollow Abbot, who keeps the stair down. Past him is the Hollow Land and the great Orange Spire, twenty floors of it, rated from level 58 to level 65. Every floor has a mystery room (a riddle, a bridge, a pit, a chasm and other tests), and some walls are hidden doors that open when you walk into them. On a riddle floor the wizard's right answer heals the party and pays XP and gold.

The Spire's own people are the Armoured Goblin, the Cave Slime and the Orc Brute, and from the third floor one or two new horrors join them every second floor, until the Flesh Abomination on the nineteenth. A Hollow Land creature stands one level above its place unless the entry says otherwise.

The Wandering Monsters

Fifteen strange creatures turn up on a level without warning, about one visit in six to a suitable place. They have no nests and no graves. Each one is written into the Wandering Monsters page (Menu, More) the first time you meet it, with its picture, its legend and its health, and the page has an About them button with the old people's account of where they come from.

Nine of them have a favour to ask and so start a quest: bring them what they want and they pay in gold, experience and a keepsake. Five fight, and each of those carries magic gear and stands one level above the party. And one, the Messenger of Death, is the rarest and the worst.

Part Two: The Hint Guide

The old hint books of the cartridge days were written in three kinds of ink. This one follows the habit. Every entry begins with a nudge, a line of plain advice that spoils nothing. If the nudge is not enough, the stronger hint in the first dashed box points at the place or the person. The answer in the second box tells you straight out.

Read only as far as you need. Every hint in this book was checked against the game as it is played, and none of them is a guess. Those boxes marked SPOILER give away the story as well as the solution, so a first-time player may choose to read the nudges only.

The game has a hint of its own too. In the quest log (J) every quest that is more than a plain fetch or a kill count has a Hint button, and tapping a quest circles its place on the map. A quest ready to hand in always says who to hand it to. Hints you press are counted on the Stats page.

General Advice for New Players

Rest and food

Rest with R. It takes eight hours, heals every hero to full and refills mana or stamina, and a fallen hero rises with half health. You cannot rest while enemies are close. Camp is also where the party cooks, sharpens, tends the sick and tells stories, one choice for each hero each day.

Eat. Each hero's hunger bar falls by about a quarter in six hours, and the game tells you when a hero drops below 25. A hero at zero loses a point of health every twenty minutes, and a starving hero only heals to half when the party rests. Resting at an inn heals the whole party fully even if they are hungry, and lifts hunger to at least 70. Two food items cooked at camp make a stew: Well Fed, +2 strength for a day.

Trading

A shop pays 40 per cent of an item's price. Each town keeps its own opinion of the party, from minus 100 to plus 100: honest quests raise it, warnings from the watch and the shady jobs of each town's fixer lower it. A liked party pays up to 22 per cent less, a hated one pays more, and a despised one is refused. Persuasion takes 1 per cent off per level, and Appraisal adds 1.5 per cent to what you sell for.

Saving

The game saves itself (Autosave) and keeps the previous two runs. You also have a quick save on F5 and three slots of your own in the Save and Load page of the menu. Save before a door you have a feeling about.

Potions, powers and weight

H or P drinks the frequent-use potion. In the bag you can drag two more potions on to the backup slots, and when the first runs out the next steps up in its place. X uses the chosen hero's special attack or spell. Powers cost stamina or mana, and a rest refills both.

Every item has a weight. The party can carry 20 kilograms plus 1.6 for every point of the heroes' combined strength, and has 36 pack slots plus one for every two points of strength. Over the limit it moves at half speed and fights worse, and cannot lift anything more from the floor, a chest or a shop. A Ring of the Pack Mule (eight slots, 15 kg), a Ring of the Porter (20 kg), a Ring of the Great Haul (16 slots, 30 kg), a donkey (12 slots, 30 kg) or a llama (10 slots, 20 kg) all help.

Which hero does what

Every hero chooses one of twelve classes when the party is made. How much the party can carry depends on the heroes' strength added together, so the strong classes (Warrior, Barbarian, Paladin, Priest, Monk and Goat) help everyone. Each class has one special attack, used with X.

Hidden Caves, Wild Dungeons and the Green Crosses

Behind the trees of the outdoor lands are caves and old overgrown ruins that no road leads to. Their doors are choked with dead, twisted trees, and a small ? appears on the map when the party has looked towards one. The mark goes when you have been inside. Walk slowly and keep your eyes open: a Ranger in the party sees them from much further off, and so do a Scout and the herb trades (Healer, Alchemist).

Watch your minimap for a ?

Hidden caves and wild dungeons lie off the roads, behind the trees. When your party looks towards one, a ? appears at the edge of the minimap and on the area maps. Follow the mark. It goes away once you have been inside.

Dead trees are the game's burnable ones. Tap one with a torch in the bag and it burns for five seconds, then it is ash you can walk through. A torch is used up. A lantern does the same and is kept. Two people in the towns will tell you more: a woodsman in Thornwick and a scout in Ironhill talk about the hidden places at length.

Finding the hidden places

Nudge: Walk straight into the woods from the roadside now and then, and look for a few dead, twisted trees standing in the open in front of bare rock.

Stronger hint. Bring a torch or a lantern. On the Pilgrim's Path, look beside the road for old stone crosses green with moss, and turn east at one.

The answer. A mossy cross stands beside the road on the same row as the entrance to a cave or a wild dungeon. Turn east at the cross and walk straight on, and you will arrive. (An old soldier in Oldhaven will tell you as much.) Elsewhere on the Path only about three entrances in ten are hidden behind thickets; the rest stand in plain sight.

The caves of the old forests

There are seven caves in the older lands, each with its own name: The Witch's Hollow in the Thornwood, The Bear Den in Gandrick's Wood, The Hunted Man's Hole on the Ironhill Road, The Moor Wolves' Cave on Ironhill Moor, The Exile's Cave in the Greyfang Pass, The Greendale Hoard and The Oakwood Bear Cave. Three have quests, and they are in the quest tables below.

The caves of the Pilgrim's Path

Twenty small, dark caves are hidden along the Path, one in nearly every stretch. They are named: The Wolf Hollow, The Root Cellar, The Outcast's Den, The Glittering Crack, The Fugitive's Cave, The Old Smuggler's Hole, The Grey Den, The Gem Crack, The Hermit's Burrow, The Runaway's Cave, The Buried Hoard, The Howling Cave, The Crystal Hollow, The Ragged Man's Cave, The Moss Chest Cave, The Hunted Man's Cave, The Glimmer Pocket, The Wolf Mother's Den, The Beggar's Cave and The Forgotten Strongbox. Some hold wolves, some treasure or gems, some an outcast. Three hold a runaway who has a quest.

The overgrown ruins

Every outdoor map has from one to four old ruins, and the Pilgrim's Path has one or two in each stretch: vine-grown stone in different colours, with trees and flowers growing inside, such as The Ivy Chapel, The Mossy Vault and The Tangled Crypt. About seven in ten are empty and dark. About one in six holds a little treasure, and about one in ten has been taken by slimes. All are rich in herbs.

Inside, the way out is a bright arch of daylight behind you, and the exit is always marked on the map. Caves and ruins also have wild food on the floor, such as chanterelles, wild figs and even truffles, and now and then a gem. About four chests in ten hold one piece of armour, and now and then a powerful one.

Quest Hints, Place by Place

The big quests of each place come first, each in three levels. The long lists of small errands follow in tables.

Thornwick and the Thornwood

Kill Gandrick (Mayor Corbin)

Nudge: Leave Thornwick by the east gate. The danger comes from the woods beyond.

Stronger hint. Follow the trail through Gandrick's Wood, fighting the wolves and bandits on the way. His cave is at the far end of the wood.

The answer. His cave is at the far east end of Gandrick's Wood. Kill him: he can curse a hero and shake the party, so a church can lift what he leaves. Tap the quest in the log to see it on the map.

The Lost Goats (Old Maud)

Nudge: Goats do not hide in the wild. Look close to home.

Stronger hint. They are in the lanes and yards of Thornwick itself.

The answer. Five goats are loose in the town. Walk right up to one, or tap it, to find it. Then tell Maud.

The Empty House (Nim)

Nudge: Haunted houses are dark. Prepare for that.

Stronger hint. The house is on the north side of the main street in Thornwick.

The answer. Go in with a torch and kill the three ghosts inside. The house is too quiet and can leave the party shaken.

Ironhill and the Moor

Hesta's Pick

Nudge: Look in the mines. The tunnels are wide and a pick is small.

Stronger hint. It lies on the floor in the south-west part of the mines, in the lower left quarter of the map.

The answer. The mines are east of Ironhill castle. The pick lies on the floor in the lower left quarter of the mines. Twelve pieces of iron ore lie about the mines too, and Brannoch only needs six: take them for him on the same trip.

The Overseer (Foreman Bryn)

Nudge: The Overseer waits at the very end of the mines. Do not go in hungry.

Stronger hint. He is in the deepest room, far to the south-east, behind a last door.

The answer. Mine Overseer: 210 base health, level 25, hits hard. Rest and heal before you open the last door. He is a named foe and is never made weaker than his place.

Out of the Dungeons

Nudge: You cannot walk out. But you may not have to fight everything.

Stronger hint. The jailer will take a bribe. The guards beyond will not.

The answer. Bribe the jailer, fight your way out past the guards and deal with Halbrecht in the castle dungeons (his base health is 185).

Wynn's herbs and Eadric's parcel

Nudge: One errand is out on the moor, the other too.

Stronger hint. Moor herbs grow on the moor, east of town; Eadric lives on the moor. Pip, the lost boy, is on the moor too.

The answer. Pick six moor herbs from the ground for Wynn. Take Dorn's parcel to Farmer Eadric first, then tell Dorn. Find Pip near the wolves and tell him to go home.

The fixers, and a word about honour

Each of the four old towns has a fixer who offers two shady jobs for good gold: Sly Dunstan in Thornwick, One-Eyed Karl in Ironhill, Whisper Mirelle in Anaea and Khafre the Fixer in Khemetra. One asks you to lean on a merchant ("A Quiet Word"), the other to sell stolen goods to a shopkeeper who asks no questions ("No Questions Asked"). Each pays 100 to 155 gold, and each lowers the town's opinion of the party. The prices of every shop in that town follow.

Stronger hint. Walk the beach of Yarrim, north and south of the pier.

The answer. Four crates lie in the sand along the beach. Tell Tamsin Kell when you have all four. For her second job, kill six rats in the Net Loft, east of the harbourmaster's office.

The Lamp of Yarrim (Orsa)

Nudge: The lighthouse has no fuel for its great lamp. Think what lights things.

Stronger hint. Three torches. Wen's Trading Post in Yarrim sells them, and so do Khemetra's general stores.

The answer. Bring Orsa three torches at the Yarrim Light. Orsa pays 245 gold and a potion, and you will want that lamp when you run the reef at night with a black ship on your tail.

Four islands, three pages (aboard the Dawn Walker)

Stronger hint. Saltbarrow (Sable Maeve), the Isle of Bells (Brother Aldous), Cinder Isle (Ketra) and Last Light (Isolde).

The answer. Saltbarrow: kill ten of Corvane's marines, camped along the north and east shore, and tell Sable Maeve. Cinder Isle: take three cinder crystals from the slopes and destroy the Cinder Guardian that walks them. The Kraken Guns

The Kraken Guns (Menkaure)

Stronger hint. Ketra, Sable Maeve, Brother Aldous and Isolde each know where one lies. Every hero needs one in hand.

Making Money

  • Gems. Forty kinds lie about on empty squares of the dungeons, the better ones deeper down. A gem sells for 40 to 300 gold.
  • A shovel. Press G on an empty square. A Peasant's Shovel (30 gold) finds something about one dig in a hundred; the King's Golden Shovel (7000) one in ten. Finds are coins, gems, boots, weapons and oddities.
  • Butterflies. A cage costs 20 gold. A rare one is worth much more than a common one.
  • Houses. About one door in three is locked: buy lock picks from any trader (25 gold). Some houses have coins in a corner. Khemetra's watch warns anyone seen walking into a house and jails them on the third warning.
  • Rumours. Three hoards are rumoured in Khemetra: a curse-heavy chest of 5000 in the far north-west of the Old Quarter, 2000 in the north-east behind a curtain in the Temple District, and 1000 in the far south-east near the baths under a table.
  • The Golden Pyramid. In the Golden Erg. At the heart of its maze lies a chest with a thousand gold, and the Undying Pharaoh walks the maze.
  • The big quests. The Court of King Osric pays 1265 gold, Vance's Letters 1265, the Hollow Abbot 1665 and the Question at the Top 2465.

Hard Fights and How to Survive Them

Rest first. Heal and refill every hero before a boss door. A last door is a door you cannot go back through as a stronger party, so use the stair, rest at the inn and come back. If a place is too hard for the party, go elsewhere and come back stronger: the fixed world was made for exactly this.

Know what a great creature is. They stand above their place by a fixed amount: Cinderback three, the Cinder Guardian two, the Hollow rulers three, the Hollow Abbot four, the Messenger of Death five, and the Kraken twenty. You cannot win these fights by brute force before the level is right.

Use the undead rules. A Paladin's Smite doubles against skeletons. Spectres, ghosts and wraiths haunt and shake the party, and the church lifts a curse.

In the Orange Spire, kill the vampires, abominations and Pale Lord quickly, because they heal. Beware chests that look old, and strangers who look pale.

The Messenger of Death

One level in twenty-five has him. He touches the party once, and every hero is left shaken and one is haunted, and then he runs and hides. He has hidden the five letters of his name, D, E, A, T and H, on the walls of the level. Every letter the party reads is written in a book. When all five are read, the party is dead.

What the code lets you do: he can be killed, and he is only 70 base health, five levels above the party. After about twenty-two rounds of lurking he rushes at the party. Once he is hurt he fights back. He drops The Last Word, a long black sword that does 20 to 29 damage and has never missed. The letters read so far show at the top of the view, and a letter once read stays read. Each letter is scrawled flat on a wall, at least seven squares from the next, and counts as read when it comes into view within about six squares. Find him first.

Cheat Sheet: the Level Ladder and Autoscaling

Autoscaling off (the default). The world is fixed. Every place has its own level, rising by about three for each place the story leads to, and the world map shows the level next to every place. Creatures stand at the level of the place (some a little lower), and the great creatures a few above it, by at most eight. If a place is too hard, go elsewhere and come back stronger.

Autoscaling on. Creatures scale to the party. A party that has outgrown a place meets creatures raised to within a few levels of itself, and the great creatures always stand above the party. A place no longer gets easy as the party grows. Turn it off again whenever you like: the world goes back to its fixed levels.

Hint: To change it, open the menu, then Settings, then Gameplay, and press the Autoscaling button. The page tells you the level of the place you are standing in, or that the place has no fixed level.

Common Questions

I cannot rest. Enemies are close. Move away from them, or kill them. An inn lets you rest in any case.

A hero keeps losing health for no reason. Check hunger (zero hunger costs a point of health every twenty minutes) and then the Afflictions page. Poison, fever and bleeding all wear a hero down.

It says the load is too heavy or the bag is full. There are two limits, slots and weight. Drop or sell something, or add a ring, a donkey or a llama.

A door will not open. It is locked. About one house door in three is. Buy lock picks, and note that a pick can bend or snap, so buy a few.

How do I cure a curse? In a church or temple. The Church of Light in Khemetra will lift it.

What is the gold ! and the green ? A gold ! over someone means a quest to give. A green ? means someone to whom a finished quest can be handed in.

What is the ? on the map? A hidden cave or ruin that the party has seen but not yet entered.

Where did the Ferryman (or another wanderer) go? They stay where you met them. The quest log names the place.

How do I get a Diving Helm? Buy one for each hero from Tam Salvor on the pier at Yarrim.

Can I save whenever I like? Yes: the quick save on F5, and three slots, and the game saves itself as well.

The place is too hard. Go elsewhere and come back. Or turn autoscaling on.

I am lost. Tap the quest in the log: its place is circled on the map. Press Hint for words.

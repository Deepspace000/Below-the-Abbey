Chapter 4The History of the Land

Rats in the crypt, wolves in the wood, a ledger, a warrant and a sea with something in the middle of it: how your four heroes came to be where they are.

How to read this chapter

This chapter is a history book for the lands of the game, in the manner of the old "Story" chapters in the boxes of the games we grew up with. It is written in layers.

  • The Tale So Far and The Lands You Will Walk tell you only what you learn in your first hour. They are safe for everyone.
  • After the warning page, every section is marked SPOILER. It tells the whole story of the journey, including who betrays whom, and it names the places the game saves for the end. If you are new, stop at the warning and come back when you have played.
  • The game keeps the same story for you in the journal, on the page called The Story So Far. This chapter is that text with the people and the places put around it.

The Tale So Far

The tale begins in the dungeons under the Abbey. Four adventurers went down into the crypts to kill the Rat King, who ruled the vaults with an army of rats. They fought through the halls and the bones, found the Rat King in his lair, and killed him. Then they climbed out of the crypt into the daylight.

That is where you take the party. You are not told who the four are or who sent them. They are simply the people who went down, and came up again.

The Abbey above was empty and silent, and an old dark forest crowded against its gate. There is nobody to ask what happened to the brothers. The first thing that does happen is the road: on the trail through the woods the party is set upon by wolves.

They kill them, but it is not a wild hunt. The wolves circle, wait and strike together, and the party works out that somebody sent them. A band of bandits attacks soon after, and that settles it. Someone wanted these four dead before they reached the road.

The road leads to Thornwick, a small timber town behind a log palisade. The streets are almost empty, and people watch from doorways. The party goes to the mayor, Corbin. He tells them that the raids on the road are the work of a bandit chief named Gandrick, who sent the wolves and the bandits against them, and he asks the party to go and kill him.

That is the whole of the opening. What the party finds in Gandrick's wood is for you to discover.

The Lands You Will Walk

The world map in the game (open the map screen and press the World map button) shows two great pieces of country with a sea between them. In the west lie the Known Lands. In the middle lies the Narrow Sea, and its islands. In the east lie the Eastern Lands, called on the map The Far Lands, where the king's city stands. The two shores are joined by a long road in the far north, and by nothing else that is safe.

The Known Lands

The party begins at The Abbey, in the Thornwood. East of it lies Thornwick, the timber town, and beyond it Gandrick's Wood and a cave in the hillside. A muddy road runs through the forest to The Ironhill Tunnel and out to Ironhill, a stone town under a castle, with a burial ground, mines and a moor around it. East of the moor the land climbs to the Greyfang Pass.

Beyond the pass lies Greendale, a green valley with a white town called Anaea. The map calls everything east of the pass "the unknown lands". North of Anaea the country turns to sand: the Sand Road, the Red Dunes, the Blue, White, Golden and Violet deserts, and the golden city of Khemetra.

The Narrow Sea

The road ends at the water. At the far end of a tunnel lies the fishing town of Yarrim, and out on the sea are four islands: Saltbarrow, the Isle of Bells, Cinder Isle and Last Light. Under each island there is a reef, and over the whole of it lies the open sea, a hundred cells of it, which you sail yourself.

The Eastern Lands

On the far shore stands Oldhaven, "the king's city, across the Narrow Sea", with the Oakwood behind it. A quiet road called the Pilgrim's Path leaves its north gate and runs the whole way round to a cliff above Yarrim.

Stop here if you are new

Everything after this page is a spoiler. It tells the story of the whole journey, from the ledger to the Kraken to the end of the road, and it gives away who is not to be trusted.

Play the game first. Turn the page when you have reached the end of what you can bear not to know.

Ledgers and Mayors

Spoiler: this section tells what happens in Gandrick's wood, in Thornwick and in Ironhill.

East of Thornwick the forest is older and darker. No birds sing. Bones lie along the trail, and black-coated figures move between the trees. The party fights through wolves and bandits and finds the cave in the hillside, where cold air breathes out smelling of wet stone and old death. The dead have been laid down there for a long time, and now the living guard them.

The party kills Gandrick, and his black raven shrieks once and flies out into the dark. Among his things is a leather ledger: page after page of payments in silver, each marked with the letter C. The last entry reads, "Forty crowns to C. for sending fools. Four more went into the wood today." The C is Corbin. The mayor of Thornwick has been taking a cut from the bandit all along, and sent the party into the wood because he thought four strangers would die like all the rest.

The party goes back and lays the ledger before him. He goes grey, does not reach for the book, and reaches for the sword on his wall instead. He tells them they were meant to die, and that now they will not leave the room. The party fights him in his own hall, and he falls to his knees and drops his sword.

The King's Captain

As Corbin falls, horns sound in the square and soldiers in the king's colours push into the hall. Their captain looks at the mayor, then at the ledger. The raids on that road had been too well planned, he says, and the crown has come to find out who was behind them. They cuff Corbin in iron and lead him away to the king's dungeons. The captain asks one more thing: Ironhill is the next town, and its mayor must hear what happened here, in case he too dealt with Gandrick. The way leads west through a haunted wood and a cave under the hill, and out beneath the stone walls of Ironhill.

Mayor Halbrecht

Mayor Halbrecht already knows what happened: news travels fast, even through the old tunnel. He congratulates the party and asks a favour. Undead, rats and bats have come up out of the old mines east of the castle, and he wants eight of them killed.

The party clears the mines and goes back for its pay. This time Halbrecht listens, and his smile slowly goes away. A man from Thornwick has told him that the party arrived with a ledger nobody but they had seen, and he accuses them of forging it, to ruin the mayor of Thornwick and take his road. "I do not pay forgers." He calls his guards and has the party thrown into the castle dungeons.

The Dungeon, the Warrant and the Pass

Spoiler: the escape from Ironhill and the flight into Greendale.

Iron cuffs, a long stair, a cold cell, and guards laughing outside the door. The party bribes the jailer, fights out past the guards and faces Halbrecht. As he falls he admits it: he and Corbin split the coin, two mayors on one road, and Gandrick kept every other town poor. He could not let them tell the king.

Captain Rowan comes down with six guards, says he has served the wrong man for six years, and takes the mayor into custody. But he warns the party that Halbrecht sent a rider to the king at dawn, naming them as forgers. By now there is a warrant for all four of them, and the hunters and hitmen who live off warrants are already on the road. The party are fugitives.

Rowan can stop the hunters in town, not on the moor, so he sends the party up the Greyfang Pass, cold and steep, where horses cannot follow. With the hunters at their heels they cross it and leave the known country behind. This is Greendale, which the king never bothered with.

The Green Valley and the Golden City

Spoiler: Anaea, Khemetra, the wizard and the king.

Anaea is a white town with yellow trim in a green valley, and the people in the square are every colour at once: a woman with blue skin and orange hair, a boy green from head to foot. Mayor Marigold has not seen travellers come down the Greyfang road in years. She hears the whole story and says it is a sorry tale and that she believes it, because liars tell it better.

The king has not sent a tax collector or a soldier to Greendale in twenty years, and would not listen to a village mayor. But there is one man he does listen to: the wizard Ishkander, who once saved the king's life.

The road runs north across the Sand Road and the desert to Khemetra, the golden city, four hundred thousand souls of whom not one has heard of them. The grand vizier, Hotep-Ra, will not give the wizard's hiding place to strangers, but he names it for those who recover the royal seal.

The Wizard in the Cistern

Ishkander lives in a study at the end of the cisterns beneath the Potters Market. He knew the warrant was forged the moment he heard the word "forger", and he promises to write to the king, who is away at his summer court. He warns the party that the warrant carries a price, twenty thousand crowns on each head, and that such money buys knives.

The King's Audience

Trumpets sound over Khemetra. King Rahotep is home. He reads Ishkander's letter and the dead bandit's ledger, and knows that the hand that signed the warrant is not King Osric's. It is Lord Corvane, Warden of the western Crown, who has been taking the tolls for himself. The ledger the party carries is only a copy. The master was written in three pages by Corvane's scribe, Isolde, who fled by sea and hid them on the islands of the Narrow Sea.

Then a dying rider falls at the foot of the throne: Corvane's black ship, the Black Heron, lies off Yarrim, and he means to have the party by the new moon. The king orders the Sea Gate unsealed. The warden's key lies in a rusted chest in the cisterns, and with it the grand vizier opens the gate in the east wall of Khemetra, shut for a hundred years.

The king wants the party out of the city by a way the warden's men will not be watching: the sea. The tunnel behind the gate has been sealed for a hundred years, and it is full of the drowned dead. Menkaure the shipwright goes with them, and when it is cleared he calls it a road at last. It leads east, under the cliffs, to the sea.

The Narrow Sea

Spoiler: Yarrim, the islands, the diving, the Kraken and the crossing.

At the far end of the tunnel lies Yarrim, a small fishing town with gulls over the pier and boats moored in the bay. The harbourmaster, Tamsin Kell, is hiding the Dawn Walker, a ship built in secret, from Corvane's men, whose black ship lies off the point.

The Dawn Walker slips her moorings on the evening tide just as the Black Heron turns into the bay. Corvane calls for them to heave to. The party runs for the shallows by the light of the lamp, and by dawn there is only the sea.

The Three Pages

Saltbarrow
A pirate island of sand and wrecks. Sable Maeve wants Corvane's marines gone from her shore. The party clears them, and she tells them where Isolde has gone.
The Isle of Bells
A drowned abbey. Brother Aldous will give up the first page of the ledger only to those who ring all three bells and face the dead who wake.
Cinder Isle
A black volcano. The ash-priestess Ketra asks for three crystals and the death of the Cinder Guardian. She gives up the second page.
Last Light
Isolde sits in a hut by a ruined beacon. The third page lies in the altar at the top of the island, and Lord Corvane has followed the party's lamp across half the sea. He does not leave the island.

Below every island there is a reef. Tam Salvor of Yarrim sells diving helms, and the water is full of colour and teeth: coral gardens, drowned abbeys, vents that breathe, and a wreck among the kelp. The party has four minutes of air at a time, and the bubbles at the vents fill the helms again.

On the Isle of Bells Brother Aldous tells of a great spiral shell, a nautilus grown over a thousand years, that broke in four in a storm and sank, one piece beneath each island. The party dives all four underwater dungeons. Aldous lays the pieces on the stone beside the abbey bell, and they close together by themselves with a sound like the sea. The shell becomes the Nautilus Helm, pearl-white inside and rose at the lip.

The Kraken Guns

With the whole ledger on the chart table, Menkaure shows the party the way to Oldhaven, across the Narrow Sea, and the thing that stands in the middle of it. The Kraken is twenty levels above the party, and nothing they carry can hurt it. The old Crown fleet killed it once with four magical harpoons, the Kraken guns, which were scattered when the fleet burned.

Ketra, Sable Maeve, Brother Aldous and Isolde each know where one lies: the red cliffs of Redcliff, the wreck on Kelpgate Rock, the standing stones of the Hollow Isle and the wreck under Last Light. They are the Ember Harpoon, the Verdigris Harpoon, the Silver Harpoon and the Beacon Harpoon. Each hero takes one, and they do huge damage to the Kraken and next to none to anything else.

East of the line where the gulls stop, the sea goes black and the Kraken rises, a thousand suckers breaking the surface at once. The four harpoons bite into it as nothing else in the world could, and it sinks. They flare white and fall to ash, as they were meant to. For the first time in a century the way across the Narrow Sea is open, and the harbour boom at Oldhaven can be lifted.

The Eastern Lands

Spoiler: Oldhaven, the court, the six names and the road back to the Abbey.

The Dawn Walker crosses the Narrow Sea and comes at dawn to Oldhaven, the king's city, with the whole master ledger in Isolde's own hand. Here the second part of the story begins: the western lands, where the road began, lie far behind across the ocean.

At noon the party is brought before King Osric and his court. The ledger is read in open court. It shows that Lord Corvane, Warden of the Narrow Sea, took the tolls in the king's name and had the warrant forged, and that at the foot of the last page he names Chancellor Vance, who keeps the king's seal and gives the orders. Vance draws a sword and falls on the steps of the dais. King Osric tears up the warrant: it carries his seal, but he never signed it, because Vance held the seal and Corvane wrote his name. The ransom on four heads is lifted.

But Vance and Corvane did not work alone. In the margin of the ledger are six more names: six people at the court and in the city who took Corvane's silver or carried Vance's orders. King Osric cannot tell which of his own court he can trust, so he asks the party, who owe the city nothing, to find the six quietly.

The Six Pieces, the Siege and the Oak Crown

Captain Aldwyn matches the six initials to six people at the fair of Saint Brannoch. All six were paid with one piece of a larger thing: a brass cog, a steel pin, a bronze spring, a silver bell, a lens of green glass and a pale dial. The party chooses mercy or justice for each.

Then the Ash Guard seizes the east gate and holds the road beyond it. The city holds out.

Beyond the gate lies an old oak forest with six glades. The woodwardens keep it, under Hale. The Oakwrights plant an oak for every one they cut, under Master Joss. A young woodwarden named Wren tells the party she is King Osric's granddaughter, raised in the Oakwood because the chancellor could not be trusted. At the Great Oak, Ysolt asks three questions, and the party answers honestly.

Home to the Abbey

With Wren crowned, Captain Aldwyn reads Ishkander's sealed letter aloud. The six pieces fit together into the Orrery of Hours, a clockwork model of the heavens, which opens a sealed door under the Abbey crypts, where the journey began. A royal cutter takes the party home. In the crypt, near the old stair, stands a slab of black iron with a ring of hollows in the middle. The Orrery ticks, the discs turn, and the door grinds into the wall. A stair goes down into the dark.

Four floors lie below the Abbey: the Sealed Stair, the Drowned Cloister, the Ossuary of Brothers and the Hollow Abbey. At the bottom stands Brother Sevrin, the Abbey's last gardener, who has come down to hide. He tells the party that the Hollow Abbot sits in the great room ahead, and that the stair onward is under his seat.

Below the Abbey is in early access

When the Hollow Abbot is killed, the slab over the stair grinds aside. Beyond it lies the Hollow Land, a vast underground dark with a great orange spire of twenty levels, and past the spire the first of the towers beyond. The game says plainly that this is where the road ends for now. You can read the whole of what is written so far in the journal.

The Deepest Secret

Spoiler: who Vance served. Read this last.

Brother Sevrin tells the party what the Hollow Abbot was guarding. Long ago a court of dead rulers was shut under the Abbey, and the Abbey kept the door. Vance, the chancellor, was never the master of Corvane's silver. He answered to someone in the Hollow Land, and he spent the silver to open the door from outside.

In the hidden rooms of the Orange Spire the party finds three letters from Vance to his lord. Corvane's silver went up to the Hollow Land every quarter. Vance used the king's seal and the king saw nothing. He hid six pieces of the key in six hands, so that only the court's servants would open the door.

At the top of the Orange Spire the wizard tells the party the truth. He shut the court of the Hollow Land under the Abbey long ago, ruled by the first king, whom they call the Pale Sovereign, who wanted to rule past his own death. Vance was the Sovereign's hand above, Corvane was Vance's tool, and the silver bought the pieces of the key. The party opened the door themselves, and that is the only reason the court is not loose in the Eastern Lands. The Sovereign waits in the Violet Tower.

The Pilgrim's Path and Saint Elen's

Spoiler: the quiet places of the Eastern Lands.

A second gate in the north wall of Oldhaven opens on the Pilgrim's Path, a quiet road of ninety-six kilometres that runs north up the peninsula with the sea on one side and an old oak forest on the other, turns west across the mountains and comes south to a high cliff above Yarrim. Nothing happens on it. It goes one way: from Oldhaven to Yarrim. The path ends at the top of a cliff, and a steep goat track leads down to the green slopes north of Yarrim. It can be climbed down but not up again, and a wooden sign at the bottom says so.

Halfway along, in the mountains, a gate cut into the cliff leads into the green grounds of Saint Elen's Monastery: a church, a cloister with a fountain, a refectory, a scriptorium, a kitchen, a guest house, an infirmary, herb and flower gardens, an orchard and hives. The brothers are friendly and quiet. In a chest in the old crypts the party finds the plain gold ring of Abbot Aldhelm and brings it back to Abbot Anselm, who puts it where it belongs.

Who's Who

Spoiler: every figure here has a part in the story above.

Odd Folk of the Road

Strange things walk the levels without warning. The in-game page "About them", in the Wandering Monsters list of the main menu, says that they are not born and do not live anywhere: "the things that were dreamt so hard that they got up and walked out of the sleeper's head and into the world." Fifteen are known. Nine ask a favour and six hunt you. The rarest of the hunters, the Messenger of Death, writes the letters of his name on the walls.

A Glossary of the Land

Crowns
The coin of the land. The price on each of the party's heads is twenty thousand crowns.
Dawn Walker
The ship built in secret at Yarrim, which you take to sea.
Greendale
The green valley beyond the Greyfang Pass. The king never bothered with it.
Kraken guns
Four magical harpoons that hurt the Kraken and next to nothing else.
Narrow Sea
The water between the western and eastern lands, with four islands and a reef under each.
Nautilus Helm
The helm made from the four pieces of the Singing Shell. It heals its wearer every round of a battle, brings mana back faster, and lets them breathe under the sea.
Orrery of Hours
A clockwork model of the heavens made from six pieces. It opens the sealed door under the Abbey.
Pilgrim's Path
A road of ninety-six kilometres from Oldhaven to a cliff above Yarrim.
Scoria
The desert kingdom in the north, whose golden city is Khemetra.

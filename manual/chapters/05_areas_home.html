Chapter 5Guide to the Lands: The Home Lands

From the crypt where the road begins to the orange spire in the dark below: a traveller's guide to every town, wood, mine and monastery of the west.

Every journey needs a guidebook, and this is yours. It follows the order of the road: out of the crypts beneath the Abbey, through the Thornwood to Thornwick, east to the iron town of Ironhill, over the Greyfang Pass to green Greendale and white Anaea. After that it turns back to the places a party reaches later: the long Pilgrim's Path, Saint Elen's Monastery, the Oakwood, and the strange country that lies under the Abbey itself.

For each place you will find who lives there, what they sell or ask, which creatures walk the roads, and the level of the place as the world map shows it. The deserts, the sea and the great city of Oldhaven have their own chapters (see their own chapters). Secrets are left for Chapter 8, where they belong.

Skipping ahead

The title screen has a Go to area list that starts a game in any place in this chapter, at any level of gold. It is meant for visiting, not for beating the game in an afternoon.

Reading the level

The level is a rough guide to the creatures you will meet there, with the great creatures standing a few levels above it. If a place is too hard, go somewhere else and come back stronger.

The Abbey and its Crypts

Here the tale begins. Four adventurers went down into the vaults beneath the Abbey to kill the Rat King, and every game of Below the Abbey begins with the same cold stairs. The crypts are low, damp and torchlit, with barrels, shields, old carts and bones lying where they fell.

Level: 1 to 3. Creatures: Black Rats, Cave Bats and Skeletons, and in the deepest hall the Rat King himself (the game gives him 224 hit points, a good deal for a first fight). What to find: the first coins, potions and weapons of your career, in barrels, crates and chests. Open everything, and keep your torches lit.

The Thornwood

You climb out of the crypt into an empty abbey, and an old dark forest crowds against its gate. The grounds are a graveyard with a church, a well and a little shrine, the Shrine of the First Light, where candles burn that nobody lights. Beyond the gate a trail winds through the trees.

Level: 7. Creatures: Grey Wolves that hunt in packs, Bandits and Bandit Archers in black coats (look in the branches), and wandering Skeletons. The wolves circle and wait before they strike, so keep your front line tight. Also here: the Wolves' Den, a small cave of wolves and little else, and the way on to Thornwick.

Thornwick

A small timber town behind a log palisade, where the streets are almost empty and people watch from doorways. It is the first place to rest, shop, pray and be given jobs, and it has about a dozen of them. The town is level 7 throughout, so everything here is safe to try.

Mind your purse. A boy named Nim will bump into you in the lane and walk off fast, and a man called Rough Jem asks ten gold to walk his street. Both can be talked round if you care to try.

Gandrick's Wood and Cave

East of Thornwick the forest is older and darker. No birds sing, and bones lie along the trail. Level: 10. Creatures: Bandits, Bandit Archers, Grey Wolves and Skeletons. The Woodcutters' Shrine stands here, a bell on a post hung with ribbons.

A little way off the trail is the Bandit Cache (level 10), a bandit store-room with a few guards, and in the hillside the Cave (level 13) where cold air breathes out of the rock. Skeletons, bandits and archers guard it, and at its heart sits Gandrick, who has 160 hit points and a bad reputation.

Ironhill Road and the Tunnel

The road (level 16) runs west through a rain-soaked forest. Wolves follow you, and ghosts drift between the trunks. The Wayfarers' Stone stands beside the trail, a pillar carved with spirals where travellers scratch their names. A lone Witch's Hut stands off the road (level 16): the witch sells salves, antidotes, sleeping tea, holy water, potions, a few scrolls and torches, and wants three ghosts banished.

The Tunnel (level 19) is a cave that climbs through the hill to Ironhill. Eight Skeletons and five Ghosts keep it, and the torches burn low. It is the only way through, so go well rested.

Ironhill

A stone town with a small castle and strong walls, where forge workers come in black and leave pink from the baths. The town is level 22; the castle dungeons beneath it stand at 28. Ironhill has the best shops in the west and eleven jobs for a party with sore feet. Gates lead west to the Tunnel and east to the Moor.

Its mayor, Halbrecht, welcomes the party warmly. What he asks of them, and what follows, belongs to the story and not to a guidebook.

Ironhill Mines

East of the castle the old mines run deep, and the dead have come up out of them. Level: 25. Creatures: Black Rats, Cave Bats, Skeletons and a Mine Overseer who carries a good deal of gold. The air is thick with rock dust, so expect to catch Tomb Dust.

Mayor Halbrecht wants eight of the dead cleared out, and Foreman Bryn would very much like to see the overseer gone.

The Burial Ground

Level: 29. A walled graveyard with crosses, gnarled trees and lamps. Creatures: Ghosts, Spectres and Skeletons. Find: the Gravekeeper's Niche, a shrine for those with no grave, with a hundred small stones in its alcove. Odo the gravekeeper pays for the graveyard to be put right.

Ironhill Moor

The moor is wet underfoot and midges rise in clouds. Level: 30. Creatures: Grey Wolves, Bandits and a Bandit Archer. Farmer Eadric and the boy Pip live close to the old well. The Moor Saint, a pilgrim with a staff in a spired chapel, stands on the heather, and a ring of standing stones hums when it likes you.

Off the moor are two small places. The Hermit's Cave (level 30) has a hermit who lets the party rest by his fire once a day. The Ruined Watchtower (level 30) is kept by hunters and a bandit.

Greyfang Pass

Cold and steep, where horses cannot follow. A cleared trail two squares wide runs through the pass, marked by cairns, and the area map draws it as a dashed red line. Level: 34. Creatures: Grey Wolves, Cave Bats, Bounty Hunters and Hitmen, who live off warrants. The Pass Shrine is a stone arch with a blue rune that glows when the wind turns.

Off the trail lies the Old Vault (level 34), a cold stone vault of Skeletons and Ghosts. Past the east end of the pass is the green valley the story calls Greendale, and the edge of the known lands.

Greendale and Anaea

Greendale (level 37) is a wide green valley of flowers, deer, rabbits and wild hives, and no enemies at all. The Green Sun shrine stands in a golden niche where the children leave a bun. Wild honeycomb hangs in the low trees.

Anaea (also 37) is a white town with yellow trim, and its people are every colour at once. The king has not sent a soldier or a tax collector in twenty years, and nobody minds. Mayor Marigold, in the Town Hall, will hear your story. Nothing ever happens in Anaea, apart from a goat in the temple last month.

The Pilgrim's Path

A quiet road, ninety-six kilometres long, that runs from Oldhaven to a cliff above Yarrim. It goes north up the coast, west over the mountains, south down the far coast and then up a high pass and down the other side. It takes about an hour of walking and it goes one way only. A signpost stands at every kilometre: To Yarrim, 84 km. The whole of it is level 55, but the wolves are few.

The path is made for walking, not for fighting. Benches stand beside the road, walkers and pilgrims nod to you, and a small shrine stands beside the road every two kilometres, with bread, an apple and a wedge of cheese left on it by the one before you. Take what you need, and leave something if you can.

Besides the inns and churches there are single houses along the way, a shepherd, a fisherman, a hermit, a charcoal-burner, a gardener, a beekeeper, and a last householder at the foot of the final climb. They want nothing and will tell you so. Waterfalls and rope bridges cross the streams on a few stretches, and churchyards with crosses and gravestones stand beside the churches.

The woods. The trees beside the path are real woods: narrow trails wind off between the trunks, where squirrels, rabbits and deer go about their business and bees drift over the flowers. Now and then a wolf waits on a trail. Hidden at the back of the trees are twenty small caves, with treasure, gems, wolves, outcasts, and three that shelter someone with a favour to ask. Wild caves and ruins elsewhere have their doors choked with dead trees, which a torch or a lantern will burn away, and a small ? on the map marks them until you have been inside.

Watch your minimap for a ?

Hidden caves and wild dungeons lie off the roads, behind the trees. When your party looks towards one, a ? appears at the edge of the minimap and on the area maps. Follow the mark. It goes away once you have been inside.

Hint: the path keeps a few secrets of its own. See Chapter 8.

The Monastery Gate

Halfway along the path, as it climbs the mountains, a great stone statue of Saint Elen stands at the side of the road, twice the height of a man, with her hands held out toward the mountain. The stone is dark with rain and soft with moss. At her feet someone has cut a line of letters, and a signpost beside her points up a lane to the monastery gate.

Close by, The Pilgrim's Rest is a small shrine to a saint with candles, a good place to sit, and one of the sixteen shrines of the land.

Saint Elen's Monastery

A gate cut into the rock of the pass opens on green grounds and quiet company. The monks are friendly, there is nothing to fear in the daylight, and the monastery has no level on the world map. Below the grounds, a cellar and old crypts have a ghost problem.

The grounds hold a church, a cloister garden with a fountain and benches, a refectory, a scriptorium, a kitchen, a guest house, an infirmary, the herb garden, an orchard, rows of beehives and a bell. Ten quiet quests are given here, with generous rewards.

The cemetery and the mausoleum. East of the grounds lies the Quiet Cemetery, with Sister Wilma tending the graves among the oaks and a mausoleum at the end of the row. The cellars and crypts are another matter. The Monastery Cellars are full of ghosts, with a few rats and a bat, and the Old Crypts beneath hold Ghosts, Spectres, Skeletons and a Weeping Brother. The mausoleum holds a few ghosts of its own.

The monastery also holds the Old Shrines quest: Abbot Anselm will give 1,500 gold to a party that has found them all.

The Oakwood

After the siege of Oldhaven, the king's east gate opens on an old oak forest of six glades, where the woodwardens live and the Oakwrights plant acorns and wait a hundred years. Level: 55. Creatures: a lot of Grey Wolves, and the Ash Guard.

Folk: Hale, captain of the woodwardens; Master Joss of the Oakwrights; Bramwick, the hedge-wizard; young Wren; and Ysolt, keeper of the Great Oak. Nine quests are given here, from wolves and acorns to herbs, timber and three questions put by the Great Oak. The Oakwood Altar, a mossy altar under a moon canopy, is one of the sixteen shrines.

Below the Abbey

Under the crypts of the Abbey is a sealed door, and behind it a stair that goes down four floors: The Sealed Stair, The Drowned Cloister, The Ossuary of Brothers and The Hollow Abbey. The door opens only late in the game, after the party has crossed the sea to Oldhaven and brought back the Orrery of Hours.

Level: 56 to 58. Creatures: Black Rats, Cave Bats, Skeletons, Ghosts, Spectres and, at the bottom, many Wraiths. At the very bottom sits the Hollow Abbot (2,600 hit points, a great creature indeed). Folk: Brother Sevrin, who gives the quests of the lower floors.

The Hollow Land and the Orange Spire

Past the Hollow Abbot, the stair comes out on a high ledge in a vast underground dark. Far below, lit by red and purple torches, stands a great orange spire, and stairs lead down to its gate. This is the Hollow Land. A keeper of the stair, a hooded figure, a very old grey friar and a few other lost souls talk of the place, and none of them like it. Level: 58.

The Orange Spire has twenty levels, with its number carved large on the stone. The first floor has one room, the second two, and so on up to ten rooms on the tenth; then the rooms fall away again, down to one room on the nineteenth. The twentieth has two rooms with a trapped labyrinth between them. Every floor has a strange room or alcove, and old books lie on lecterns. The spire is level 58 at its foot and level 65 at its crown, and the higher you climb the worse your company gets.

Creatures: Armoured Goblins, Cave Slimes and Orc Brutes first, then Wailing Ghosts and Clawing Hands, Mad Jesters, Chest Mimics, Raving Madmen, Wights, Murdered Peasants, The Butcher, Phantoms, Headless Knights, Banshees, Vampires, Demented Dukes, Werewolves, Skeleton Warlords, Shape Shifters, the Writhing Choir and a Flesh Abomination. On four of the levels (4, 9, 14 and 19) a twisted dead ruler holds the last hall.

At the top a wizard waits with three questions, and the floor below him has been known to give way. The foot of the Violet Tower stands beyond. Here the Hollow Land ends for now, and the game says so on a plaque.

The road leads on, to the desert and the golden city and across the sea to Oldhaven, each in a chapter of its own. For a bad day, a hard fight or an unopened door, turn to Chapter 8.

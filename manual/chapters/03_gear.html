Chapter 3Gear, Trade and Herbs

What to carry, what to wear, what to buy and what to boil: the whole business of a well kept party.

A party is only as good as its pack. The old abbey road is long, the creatures at the end of it are patient, and a sword that is two grades too light will not forgive you. This chapter tells you how to read an item, where each thing goes, how much your heroes can carry before they slow to a crawl, and how to turn gold into gear and back again.

It also covers the quieter arts: the trades that make a hero better at shopping, mending or brewing, the herbs that grow along the road, and the little roll of lock picks that opens the doors of other people's houses. Every weapon, armour piece and ring in the game is listed in the Appendix, sorted by colour. This chapter explains the rules behind those lists.

The Inventory Screen

Press I at any time on the road to open the party's bag. Press I or Esc to close it again. Keys 1 to 4 choose which hero you are looking at. The screen has three panels and a bottom bar, and the picture below shows all of them.

Left panel: the hero
Eleven slots laid out like a paper doll: arms, helm and legs on the top row; main hand, chest and off hand below them; four rings; and the second weapon. Under them the game adds up the hero's Damage, Armor, Hit chance and Light.
Middle panel: the bag
Three columns of squares that scroll. One bag is shared by all four heroes. A small number in a corner is a stack, such as seven healing potions.
Right panel: the card
With nothing chosen it shows the hero's name, class, level and experience. Tap an item and it becomes the item's card: its numbers, its requirements, its story and its price, with Equip and Discard buttons.
Gold, weight, Sort
The coin shows how much gold the party holds (gold weighs nothing). Under it are the weight carried against the weight allowed, and the bag squares used against the squares you have. Sort tidies the bag into weapons, shields, helms, body armour and so on. The key is S.
The bottom bar
Four tabs: Items I, Character C, Spells B and Powers K. Beside them is the frequent-use potion, with two small backup slots, and the red cross that closes the screen.

Equipment Slots

To equip something, tap it in the bag and press Equip (or press E). To take something off, tap the slot and press Unequip (U). You can also tap the item and then the slot you want. If the slot is full, the old item goes back into the bag, and if the bag has no room it stays where it was.

SlotWhat fits
Main handA weapon, a light, or a potion.
Off handA shield, a weapon, a light or a potion.
Second weaponA weapon or a potion, kept ready to swap.
Head, Chest, Arms, LegsThe matching piece of armour, one of each.
Rings (four)Any ring. Four rings at once.
Spell slots (four)Spell scrolls. Mages and Sorcerers only.

Armour adds up: the helm, chest, arms, legs, shield and any ring that gives armour all count towards the hero's Armor number.

On the game screen, two small windows sit beside the special attack button. They show the off hand and the second weapon. Tap one and it swaps places with the main hand at once, so an archer can change to a dagger when a creature closes in. A potion kept in either window is drunk with a single tap, and the next one from the bag takes its place.

Hint: A hero who cannot use an item will tell you why. "Aldric needs STR 22 to use the Executioner's Blade" means exactly what it says. Train, level up, or put on a ring of strength.

Reading an Item

Every item has a card. Read it from the top:

  • The name and its kind in brackets: SWORD, CHEST, RING, SPELL.
  • The numbers. Damage is the range of a normal blow. Armor is how much it turns aside. Hit is a bonus or penalty to your chance of landing a blow. A bow also shows its Range in squares, a staff its Spell power, a potion what it Heals, a food what it Fills.
  • Special lines such as "10% to double damage" or "Items weigh 15% less".
  • Weight in kilograms.
  • Requirements: STR, DEX or INT in green if the hero has enough, in red if not. The line of story at the foot often ends "Needs level 20", and that counts too.
  • Price, the amount the item is worth. In a shop this changes to what you pay and what you would be paid (see Shops and Trade).

A note on "double damage". The card says "10% to double damage". In the game's rules a lucky blow of this kind lands half as hard again as a normal one. The card name is old; the rule is the one you will see in the combat log.

Weight and the Pack

The pack is limited in two ways at once: by the number of squares and by weight. Both grow with the party's strength, because strong hands carry more. Add up the Strength of every living hero. The bag gets:

  • 36 squares, plus half of that total;
  • 20 kg, plus 1.6 kg for every point of that total.

So a party with a combined Strength of 100 has 86 squares and 180 kg. Rings of the Pack Mule, the Porter and the Great Haul add more. A donkey adds 12 squares and 30 kg; a llama adds 10 squares and 20 kg (see the Companions page of the Journal).

Each item's weight is on its card, and the party total sits under your gold. When the load goes over the limit the number turns orange, a message appears, and the party is overloaded: it walks at half speed and fights worse (the chance to hit falls by ten points). Drop something, sell something, or put on a ring that helps. The moment the load falls under the limit the party speeds up again. A chest or shop will also refuse to hand you more than you can carry.

Weight is the quiet enemy. Four heroes, forty swords, and not one step further.

The Colours of Gear

Gear is graded by colour, and the Appendix follows the same grades. The colour is set by what the piece costs: a weapon's price is the guide, and armour pieces are scaled for their slot, since a pair of leggings is worth less than a breastplate of the same grade. Rings have a scale of their own.

White
Plain starting kit. Weapons cost 12 to 30 gold.
Green
The first real improvements. Weapons around 120 to 260 gold.
Blue
Better again, and usually with an effect of its own: bleeding, cleaving, a ward, a regrowing wound. Weapons 400 to 900.
Red
The grade for the middle of the game. Weapons 1,500 to 2,400.
Purple
Heavy steel with a strong effect. Weapons 2,600 to 5,300.
Orange
The finest pieces in the game: the four Kraken harpoons, the four boss weapons, the abbot's armour, and most pieces of the highest armour sets. Weapons 6,500 and more.

Higher grades are not simply bigger numbers. A Blue axe that cleaves may serve you better than a Red sword with nothing but damage, and a White lantern is worth more than most things in a Purple chest when the lights go out. Read the card.

Weapons and What They Do

Six kinds of weapon are carried in the game.

Dagger
Easy to hit with. Needs Dexterity.
Sword
Balanced. Needs Strength.
Axe
Hits hard, but is harder to land (a Hit penalty). Needs Strength.
Mace
Heavy, a little hard to land, and the favourite for stunning. Needs Strength.
Bow
Shoots at a target up to 3 to 6 squares ahead, depending on the bow. Needs Dexterity.
Staff
Hits for little but adds "Spell power" to every spell cast. Needs Intelligence.

The finer weapons carry an effect. The card gives it in words, and the table below explains what each one really does in a fight. A chance is rolled on every hit.

EffectWhat it does
BleedA chance to leave the foe bleeding for a few rounds. The Serrated Cutlass: a quarter of its hits make a foe bleed for 5 each round, for 3 rounds. Bleeding grows a little as the hero levels up.
CleaveEvery hit also cuts the foe standing directly behind the first, for 60% of the damage. Swords and axes only.
PierceThe bow's cleave. The arrow passes through and strikes the foe behind, for 80% of the damage.
StunA chance to knock the foe senseless: it loses its next turn. A Vanguard Claymore does this one hit in five.
Exposed (Vuln)A chance to crack the foe's guard. For a few rounds it takes half again as much damage from everyone. The Spiked Maul cracks the guard three hits in ten, for 3 rounds.
ExecuteA finishing blow. Against a foe below a set share of its health, damage is multiplied: the Executioner's Blade deals two and a half times damage to a foe under 30%. The Headsman's Axe deals triple under 25%.
Double shotBows only. A chance that a second arrow follows the first, with a full second roll of damage. The Elven Bow does it one shot in four.
ShockA chance to throw every foe within two squares to the ground, so they lose their turn. The Doom Hammer's one in five.
DazeA chance to leave the foe reeling, chilled for a round.
Boss slayerSome of the greatest weapons add extra damage against bosses only (the Crownbreaker adds a half).

The Kraken harpoons

Four orange harpoons, the Ember, the Verdigris, the Silver and the Beacon, were forged for one creature only. Each does enormous damage to the Kraken (3,000 to 4,400 a hit) and next to nothing to anything else. They cannot be bought, and every hero needs one in hand. When the Kraken is dead they crumble away.

The Great Harpoon that the chandler Pell makes from wreck-oak is a good weapon, not a Kraken gun: damage 26 to 36, needs Strength 16.

Armour, Sets and Shields

Armour comes in four body slots and the shield. Besides its Armor number, a good piece has a passive effect that is always on while it is worn. The game words them plainly:

Dodge
Enemies miss you a given percentage more often.
Regeneration
You recover a little health every few rounds.
Thorns
Every enemy that strikes you takes a few points of damage.
Ward
Every blow that lands on you is a set number of points lighter.
Life drain
You heal a share of the melee damage you deal.
Second wind
Once in thirty rounds, when below a quarter health, you heal a share of your maximum.
Power, Double
All damage rises by a percentage, or you get extra double hits.
Mana flow
Mana comes back faster.

Shields

A shield lives in the off hand and counts as armour. The Banded Round Shield and Kite Shield make enemies miss (4% and 6% more); the Spiked Shield and the Bulwark of Ages add thorns; the Dragon Shield does both. A shield means the hero is not carrying a second weapon or a lantern in that hand, so choose.

The Ten Great Sets

Ten full sets of armour, one for every ten levels from 10 to 100, each with a helm, a chest piece, arms and legs. Every piece has an effect of its own, and a hero who wears all four pieces of one set wakes the set bonus. Each piece says "Set: ...", and what all four give, on its card. Armouries sell the sets up to ten levels above your party, and chests give pieces from the best sets your party has reached.

There is one more set, with a different story. Wake every one of the twenty stone teleporters along the Pilgrim's Path, and the abbot's armour arrives in your backpack with 1,000 gold: a mitre, a chasuble of plate, cuffs and greaves. Any class may wear it, and it carries a set bonus of its own.

Rings

A hero wears up to four rings, and a ring costs little weight (0.1 kg). Rings are priced dearly in the shops, but they are the most flexible gear in the book. The kinds are:

  • Stats. Strength (Ox, Titan), Dexterity (Fox, Wind), Intelligence (Owl, Sage), all three (Champion).
  • Health and mana. Vigor, Bear, Oak; Well, Moon.
  • Fighting. Warding and Bulwark add armour; Might and Lion add damage; Keen Eye and Hawk add to hit; Duellist adds double hits.
  • Carrying. The Pack Mule adds 8 squares and 15 kg; the Porter adds 20 kg; the Great Haul adds 16 squares and 30 kg; the Featherweight makes everything the party carries weigh 15% less.
  • Trade. The Craftsman adds one rank to the wearer's trade, and the Master Hand adds two (see Trades and Skills).

Stat rings change the hero while worn, so a ring of strength can lift a hero over a requirement. Take it off and the requirement bites again.

Lights

Lights are held in a hand slot. A Torch (3 gold) gives light; a Brass Lantern (45 gold) gives a much wider, steadier light. Both are sold in most general stores. A torch or lantern in the bag will also burn down a dead, twisted tree that blocks the path: the torch is used up, the lantern is kept. The panel on the left of the inventory reads "Light none" until a hero holds one. Mages can also cast a Light spell from a scroll.

Potions, Food and Drink

The Potion button on the road (also H or P) drinks the party's frequent-use potion. In the inventory, drag a potion on to the large potion slot to make it the frequent-use potion, and on to the two small Backups beside it. When the frequent-use potion runs out, the first backup still in the bag takes its place.

Food and drink

Each hero has a hunger bar at the foot of the card in the inventory (Hunger 100%). It falls as the hours pass. Below a quarter the hero is Hungry, and at nothing, Starving. Eat by choosing the food and pressing Use (or E). Food also restores a little health.

Quest Items and Curios

Some things are not for wearing. Quest items are given or found for a particular job: firewood for an innkeeper, a letter, an urn, a parcel, planks of wreck-oak. They are marked Quest item on the card, they weigh almost nothing, and the quest log (J) says whom they are for.

The road also gives you things to collect for their own sake: herbs, gems dug up with a shovel (G) and butterflies caught in a Butterfly Cage (N). Each weighs only 0.1 kg and is kept in the bag until you hand it in, sell it, or use it, and the Collection (U) keeps a record of them. Herbs are the most useful of these, and have their own section below.

Shops and Trade

Merchants, smiths, armourers, potion sellers, scroll sellers and bakers stand behind their counters in every town, and travelling carts and caravans stop on the roads. Walk up and talk to one, choose Trade or Browse the wares, and the shop window opens.

The window has your party on the left (blue edge) and the shop on the right (gold edge). The price of each shop item is printed on its square. Tap an item to read its card.

  • Buy: tap an item in the shop and press Buy. You pay at once and the item goes into the bag. If the bag is full, or too heavy, the shop keeps it.
  • Sell: tap an item in your own bag and press Sell.
  • Sort tidies your own bag. The coin shows what you have left.

A shop pays 40 per cent of an item's price. That is for a stranger in a neutral town. Gold flows both ways, and many things move the figures:

  • Reputation. Each town keeps its own opinion of your party. A town that likes you charges up to 22% less and pays you up to 12% more. A town that dislikes you charges more, and a town that has come to hate you will not serve you at all ("We do not serve your sort here").
  • Honour. A party of good name (honour 40 or more) pays 5% less and sells for 5% more. A party of bad name pays 10% more and is paid 8% less.
  • Persuasion lowers prices by 1% per level (best 10%); Appraisal raises what you are paid by 1.5% per level (best 15%).
  • The Merchant trade: 1% either way for every rank.

What the shops keep. A general store has food, water, torches, a lantern or two and lock picks, with a few plain weapons and armour. Smiths and armourers carry the better steel, and their stock grows as the party's level does. Potion sellers stock cures, healing and the brews of battle; scroll sellers stock the three scrolls and a few rings. The herbwife and the apothecary also teach the herbal arts (see below). Stock is finite: when the last of something is bought its square goes empty, and a shop with nothing left reads "Sold out".

Travelling carts keep food, water, torches, cures and the odd bargain. Desert caravans only have water and food.

Hint: Prices are shown on the shop's squares as the price of the item, before reputation. Check what you will really pay on the card in the middle. Sell the things you no longer wear, and keep the gold for the next smith.

Trades and Skills

Open the Journal (L) and choose Skills and Trades. The page has two parts.

Four skills grow by use, up to level 10, and a hero of the matching class adds two more levels while he or she is in the party.

Trades (one for each hero)

Each hero can follow one trade. A trade has five ranks. A hero begins at rank 1 and reaches the next with 10, 30, 70 and 140 experience in the trade. A ring of the Craftsman or the Master Hand adds ranks on top, to as many as seven.

The Change Trade rule

The first time a hero takes up a trade, it costs nothing: press Take up. Afterwards the button reads Change, and every change costs 100 gold. The game asks you first ("Change trade? It costs 100 gold"). Be warned: Change does not offer a list. It moves the hero on to the next trade in this order: Healer, Smith, Scout, Cook, Alchemist, Merchant, and back to Healer. If you pass the trade you want, you pay again. The experience a hero has in a trade is kept, and it is still there if the hero ever returns to it.

Herbalism and the Herbal Remedies Page

Herbs lie in the open lands: walk over one and it goes into your bag. There are twenty-eight kinds, the rarer ones growing only in wilder, higher-level lands, and a herbalist can turn them into sixteen remedies that cure what a church would charge a great deal to cure. Herbalism is a skill of the whole party and needs no trade.

Learning it. A herbarium teaches the art. Most towns have one: the herbwife, the apothecary, the healers and the potion sellers. The first lesson costs nothing and teaches the skill and three simple remedies (Feverfew Tea, Comfrey Poultice and Lavender Calming Tea). The others cost 35 to 150 gold each, or you can find them by experiment.

The page. Once the party knows herbalism, the inventory grows a Herbal Remedies button on the hero card, whenever no item is chosen. Press it, or press Y in the inventory, and the Herbal Remedies page of the journal opens. It lists the herbs in your bag and the remedies you know.

  • Mix. Pick up to three herbs and press Mix. If they make something, you learn the recipe at once. If not, the page says "Nothing good comes of that mixture" and nothing is used.
  • Brew. Press Brew beside a known remedy. You need the herbs in the bag and a quiet moment. Brewing takes half an hour and is done at camp, away from foes. It succeeds four times in five, a little oftener for an alchemist; a failure wastes half the herbs.

Most remedies cure afflictions (see the Afflictions page, key F). Brewed remedies are cheap, a good reason to keep a herb or two in the pack.

Scrolls and Spells from Scrolls

Mages and Sorcerers do not learn spells in schools. They read them from scrolls. A scroll is an item like any other: buy it, put it in one of the hero's four spell slots (on the Spells tab, B) and the spell is ready. The slot marked Active is the one the hero will cast. No other class can use a spell scroll: the game says so if you try.

Scroll: Firebolt
A bolt of fire. 4 mana. Damage 6 to 10. Reaches 3 tiles.
Scroll: Frost Shard
Ice that also freezes the target for a turn. 3 mana. Damage 4 to 7. Reaches 3 tiles.
Scroll: Light
A glowing orb lights the way for 40 rounds. 2 mana.

A staff adds its Spell power to every spell, and the mana draughts refill the well. A mage who has no scroll in a slot has nothing to cast, so a few spare scrolls are cheap insurance. The scrolls are sold at the scroll sellers, such as the Silver Quill and the House of Seven Scrolls in Khemetra, and at the witch's shelf.

Keys and Lock Picks

Keys. In the old dungeons a rusty iron key opens one locked door: the HUD shows KEY while you hold one, and the key is used up. A golden key opens a tomb door that no iron key will.

Lock picks. About one house door in three, in any town or village, is locked. A locked door needs a real set of Lock Picks: a small roll of wrenches and hooks, 25 gold, sold by every trader. The door tells you your chance before you try. The chance rises with your Lockpicking skill and a little with your luck, and is never below 8% or above 95%.

  • Success: the door opens, but one time in four the pick is bent beyond use.
  • Failure: the pick snaps and is used up. A neighbour may hear the noise.
  • Breaking in costs a little Honour, and a neighbour who sees you will think worse of your party. The town's opinion of you counts at the shop counter, so pick your doors with care.

A hero with no lock picks cannot open a locked house at all. A Thief or Assassin in the party adds two levels of skill. Buy a few.

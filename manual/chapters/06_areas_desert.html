Chapter 6Guide to the Lands: Khemetra and the Desert

Sand roads, golden walls, sleeping sphinxes and a harbour at the end of a tunnel: a traveller's guide to the south and the east.

South of Anaea the green of Greendale thins out, and then it stops. The road you take from the east gate is the oldest in the south: it runs through a narrow forest, turns north between palms, and walks you out into the great sands. Everything in this chapter lies beyond that point. It is a country of long dunes and short tempers, of tombs that were sealed before anyone wrote down their names, and, at the end of it, of Khemetra, the Golden City, where four hundred thousand people have never heard of you.

This guide goes in the order you will most likely travel: the Sand Road and its buried chambers, the Tomb of Sut, the Red Dunes, the far deserts and their pyramid, then the city itself, district by district, and last the little harbour town of Yarrim on the Narrow Sea. Each entry gives you the level of the place (the number shown on the world map when autoscaling is off), who lives there and what they sell or ask, what hunts you, and what is worth finding.

Keep your water full and your torches fuller. The deserts are wide, the creatures are spaced far apart, and a party that wanders into the Red Dunes at level 30 will meet things that were not expecting company.

The Sand Road

The road leaves Anaea through a forest that looks, for the first mile, very like Greendale. Then the trees thin, palms take over, the palms give out, and you are walking through a desert of real dunes: the ground rises and falls under your feet, and what lies beyond the next crest is a matter of faith. At the far north of the land stands a cliff, and in the cliff is the door to the Tomb of Sut. Between the road and the cliff, sphinxes lie in pairs on either side of the way.

The Sand Road is a fixed place with a fixed level of 36. It is not a gentle place. Desert wolves hunt in packs, scorpions lie under the sand, and carrion vultures turn overhead. The nomads of the road are described in the game as desperate, and they act like it.

Who walks here

Nobody sells anything on the open sand, with one exception. Now and then (about one visit in four) a trading caravan crosses an open desert: one to four camels in a line, a rider or a load on each, and a few nomads on foot behind. They walk their route and stop when you come close. Speak to the caravan trader and you can buy water, dates, bread, cheese and apples. Caravans are found on the Sand Road, the Red Dunes and all five of the far deserts.

What to find on the Sand Road

  • Eleven mounds (burrows) in the dunes. These lead down to the buried chambers, below.
  • Five little dungeons, each with its own way in: a hatch, a small crypt, a ruin with stairs, a mound with a door, a larger crypt.
  • Obelisks marching in pairs up the road, and the two sphinxes at the head of it.
  • A low cave in the east wall of the road, leading out into the Blue Dunes.

The Buried Chambers

The mounds in the dunes are the doors to ten small dungeons, each a handful of chambers joined by corridors, with creatures in the rooms, vases and treasure on the floor, and a chest at the far end holding a piece of magic gear. Each chamber has its own plan, so no two are alike. All ten stand at level 36.

There are five kinds, each used twice. Expect to visit more than one: a good deal of the early kit of the south comes out of these chests.

The Tomb of Sut

The cliff at the head of the Sand Road hides a cave full of sand, and the cave leads down into the Hall of Eternity. Four wings open off it, and each guards a golden sun disc: the Hall of Embers, the Crypt of Sarcophagi, the Chamber of Switches and the Sand Pit. When all four discs sit in the sockets of the great door, the way opens to the throne room of Sut, Lord of the Red Desert.

The tomb is level 39, a step up from the road. Skeletons, mummies, cobras and the odd scorpion keep the corridors. The walls are carved with hieroglyphs, the ceilings glitter like a night sky, and braziers burn in every hall, which is the only reason you can see the statues of Anubis staring down at you.

When Sut falls, a door appears in the north wall, and it opens on a second desert.

Sut himself is a great creature, painted in red and gold, and he waits at the far end of the tomb. You will see him down the long hall before you reach him. Go in with full healing and the best gear you have, and see Chapter 8 for the harder questions about the tomb.

Hint: The tomb air gives a lingering cough called Tomb Dust. It is not deadly, but it wears the party down over a long visit. Do the wings one at a time and come out to rest in between.

The Red Dunes

Through the door behind Sut lies a second desert, with red sand, red rock and old ruins. The last stretch of the road is an old paved way, and far ahead to the north a city rises over the horizon. That city is Khemetra.

The Red Dunes are level 41. The wolves and scorpions of the Sand Road are here too, with more skeletons, mummies, and two kinds of nomad. Ten little dungeons lie scattered across the sand, among them the Shrine of the Sleeping Sphinx, the Barrow of the Burning Moon and the Reliquary of the Veiled Queen. Obelisks stand in pairs along the old road, and a second pair of sphinxes watches the way to the city.

The Far Deserts

A low cave in the east wall of the Sand Road leads out to the Blue Dunes, and from there the sand goes on in every direction. Five far deserts lie beyond it, each linked to the next by a low cave. All five stand at level 45, and every one is wide, quiet and sparsely populated: the creatures live a long way apart, so you may walk for minutes before anything notices you. That is not always a comfort.

Each desert has oases, rings of old ruins, bones, and a number of little dungeons whose entrances are hatches, spires, crypts and stairs. The royal cartographer in Khemetra will send you into one dungeon in each of seven deserts, from the Sand Road to the Flatlands. Ask Neferet at the palace.

The Golden Pyramid

In the Golden Erg stands a real pyramid, raised out of the sand, with a doorway in its south face. Inside is a labyrinth of golden walls. At the heart of it, behind every turning, lies a chest holding a thousand gold. The Undying Pharaoh walks the maze, ten levels above your party. He warns you when he is near, hunts you down the passages, and can warp from one part of the labyrinth to another. His servants patrol too, and their grip slows you.

The pyramid has no level of its own on the world map: the Pharaoh sets his own, by yours. Do not go in below your best.

The great creatures of the sand

Cinderback, the Fire Lizard, lies in the south of the Violet Erg. He stands at level 40, breathes a gout of flame from a distance, carries 400 gold, and drops a piece of magic gear when he falls. The Wandering Cyclops patrols the White Dunes at level 44, clubs rather than shoots, carries 500 gold, and drops magic gear of his own. Neither is hard to recognise.

Khemetra, the Golden City

Khemetra is the largest place in the game. It is a walled city, four hundred cells by four hundred, of white houses trimmed with gold, laid out round a palace whose spire seems to stand in the sky. A great avenue, nine cells wide, runs from the south gate to the palace court. Cross roads run east and west, a ring road circles the palace, and between them every kind of street, lane and dead end has been bitten into the blocks. There are lawns with palms and ponds, squares full of stalls, and a crowd that goes about its business whether you are watching or not.

The city stands at level 44, and for most of it you are in no danger at all: the streets belong to guards, porters, children and cats. The danger is in particular corners, named below. When you walk into a quarter for the first time, a line of news tells you where you are.

The Palace of Khemetra

Four hundred thousand souls and one door that matters. The palace stands in the middle of the city behind its court of white marble and gold leaf, and the guards at the gate do not blink. Walk up the avenue and into the front, and you come to the great hall: statues of kings (some of them real), braziers, guards, a page, a herald, and four nobles waiting about for something to happen. They are Lady Meret, Lord Sobek, Lady Tiye and Lord Anen, and all four will talk.

Three rooms open off the hall by their own doorways:

  • The throne room. The throne is empty at first. The guards will tell you the king is away at his summer court, and that the vizier answers for him.
  • The map room, to the west. The royal cartographer, Neferet, keeps every map of every desert here. She sends travellers to find the seven pieces of the Instrument of Dawn, one for each of seven little dungeons.
  • The vizier's study, to the east. Hotep-Ra, the grand vizier, will not give a stranger the wizard's hiding place, but he has a task for those who serve the city: the royal seal was stolen by a gang that hides in the ruined houses of the Old Quarter.

Under the City, and Out to Sea

The Cisterns beneath Khemetra (level 43) are a maze of narrow stone passages under the Potters Market, with six chambers cut into the walls. Skeletons, ghosts, scorpions, bats and a single mummy keep them: thirteen creatures in all, more than enough in the dark. Chests and potions lie in the chambers, and at the far corner of the maze a way leads up to a study.

Ishkander's Study (level 44) is a small room of bookcases, candles and cushions. The wizard Ishkander has lived here for ten years, and visitors are rare. The grand vizier will tell you how to find the hatch once you have earned it.

The Tunnel to the Narrow Sea (level 45) is a short, bright cave that ends at the shore. It is reached from the east of the Violet Erg. A second tunnel, behind the Sea Gate in the east wall of Khemetra, leads to Yarrim, below.

Zahir sells spells, every one of them real, and no one has ever been turned into a frog by accident (he says). Close by the swordsmith will sell you steel for every level of customer, from the first blade to the last. Nadira and her Iron Aegis fit helms and plate by strength and standing, so a heavy piece may need a stronger arm than you have yet.

Prices are fair and the keepers are friendly, but gold does not last. A few of the favours below pay better than the shops charge.

Work, Contracts and Favours

Khemetra has more quests than any other place. Sixty-three of them in the game's log are filed under the city, and the best way to meet them is simply to walk the streets and talk to people.

The three work houses (the Labourers' Hall, the Day Hire House and the Porters' Guild) hand out errands: fetch eight jars of water for the labourers (40 gold), six loaves for the dawn crew (36), three lanterns for the Porters (150). The goods are for sale in the markets. The Contract House and the Bounty Board sell contracts for a small fee: ten skeletons (260 gold), ten scorpions or cobras (300) or eight mummies (380). Kill them anywhere in the deserts.

Houses, Ruins and Dangerous Corners

You can walk up to almost any house in Khemetra and open its door. Sixteen ordinary ones have a painted picture of their room: a warm house with a fire and a pot of stew, a wizard's house with skulls and star charts, a magical house with glowing hieroglyphs, a cat lover's house with a small shrine to Bastet. Others hold a person with something to ask: a quiet house, a merchant's, a scholar's, a musician's. Some hold something worse.

The ruined parts of the city are marked by their names. In the Old Quarter, the Ruined House and the Collapsed House hold three skeletons each, and the Abandoned House holds two skeletons and two bats. The Old Granary is full of rats (eight, and two bats), and its keeper Gebu would like them gone. The Boarded House hides three bandits and an archer. They call themselves the Hollow Hand. The Bath Cellar holds four ghosts.

Rules of the city

  • The Beggars' Quarter and the Old Quarter are the only parts of the city that will hurt you. Go in prepared.
  • The inns, prayer houses and baths are cheap. Use them before a hard room, not after.
  • Almost any market sells water. Buy a lot before the deserts.
  • Talk to everyone twice. The first time you get the gossip, and the second, sometimes, the favour.

Yarrim, on the Narrow Sea

Once the Sea Gate in the east wall of Khemetra has been unsealed, a tunnel runs out through the rock to the shore, and at the end of it lies Yarrim: a fishing town of about twenty tiny cottages, each its own colour, round one-cell cobbled lanes, a harbour square at the root of a long pier, and a lighthouse. Gulls are everywhere. The sea is real ground, with a beach that slopes into it, islands of rock and sand, and boats moored in the water. At anchor off the beach rides the Dawn Walker.

Yarrim is level 48, and a peaceful place: the tunnel is full of the drowned dead (ten skeletons, Menkaure would like them cleared) and the Net Loft has rats (eight). Everything else is a conversation.

The people of Yarrim have small and curious troubles, and nine of them are worth a morning each: The Admiral's Telescope (Pell), The Great Crab Census (Tamsin Kell), A Message in a Bottle (Wink, a child), Wen's Left Boot, The Wedding of the Boats (Selk), Biscuit Won't Budge (Hamid and his donkey), The Net Thief of the Night Tide (Nell), The Drowned Lantern (Bessa) and The Shanty With No Last Verse (Rafe, the fiddler).

Hint: The nine small quests in Yarrim pay in gold and experience and do not need a hard fight. They are a pleasant place to rest a party that has just walked the whole length of the desert.

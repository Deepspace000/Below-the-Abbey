Chapter 1Getting Started

Four heroes, one lantern and a cold stair going down: everything you need to open the game, read the screen and take your first steps.

Welcome to the Crypts

Below the Abbey is a role playing game in the old style, made by Bickering Goat Studios. You see the world through the eyes of a party of four. You walk one square at a time, you turn a quarter turn at a time, and the game is turn based: while you are only looking, nothing happens, and when you act, the world answers.

The tale begins in the dungeons under an abbey. Four adventurers have gone down into the crypts, where the Rat King rules the vaults with an army of rats. Your game opens with the line “You wake in the crypts below the abbey. Something is scratching in the dark...” and goes on from there, out of the crypts and into a wide world of forests, towns, deserts and sea.

This chapter is the small print you need before the adventure. It tells you how to start and save the game, what every part of the screen does, how to walk, talk, open doors and chests, what lives in the menu, and how to read the maps. At the back is a page of the first ten minutes, and a keyboard and mouse chart to tear out and pin up.

What you will need

  • A web browser. The game runs in the page; there is nothing to install.
  • A mouse and keyboard, or a touch screen, or a game pad. All three work, and you can mix them.
  • Sound, if you can: there is music for every place.

Starting the Game

The itch.io page runs Below the Abbey in your browser: use the button on that page to start it. The game is also kept at its Pages address, deepspace000.github.io/Below-the-Abbey, and it is the same game. The picture is drawn at a fixed shape (three wide to two high) and grows or shrinks to fit your window, so make the window as large as you like.

Browsers keep a page silent until you have clicked or pressed a key on it, so the music begins with your first click.

The title screen

The title screen offers up to six choices.

Continue. Opens your Autosave, or the Previous run if there is no Autosave.

New game. Takes you to the choosing of your party (below).

Load game. Opens the list of saves.

Go to area. A list of every place in the game. You pick the place, your party level and your gold, and the game drops you there. It has a search box. Use it to visit places again, or to look at a part of the game you have not reached.

Settings. See the chapter’s menu section.

Exit to desktop. A browser page cannot close itself, so the game tells you to close the window or tab.

Tip: on the title screen, pressing any key does the first thing on the list: Continue if you have a game, New game if you have not.

Choosing Your Party

New game opens a screen that asks how you want your party. There are three answers.

Ready-made. Aldric the Warrior, Vesna the Thief, Orrin the Mage and Thessaly the Priest.

Random party. Four heroes are dealt for you, and you can look them over before you begin.

Make my own. You make each of the four heroes yourself.

Below those buttons you decide how the world should level. Traditional gives every place its own level: sometimes you must come back later, stronger. Scaled makes creatures grow with your party, for a smoother and slightly easier game. You are not stuck with the choice: Menu, Settings, Gameplay switches it at any time.

Making a hero

Each hero is made in three steps, and the big OK button moves you on.

  1. Appearance. Tap a portrait. The arrows at the sides turn the page to more faces.
  2. Occupation. There are twelve: Warrior, Barbarian, Paladin, Ranger, Archer, Thief, Assassin, Mage, Sorcerer, Priest, Monk and Goat. Each shows a short description and its special power.
  3. Stats. Spend the free points on Strength, Dexterity and Intelligence with the plus and minus buttons, then type a name (up to twelve letters) or press the dice for a random one.

When all four heroes stand in their slots, START lights up. The Default and Random buttons at the lower left fill the slots for you.

Saving and Loading

The game saves for you, and you can save by hand as well. Your Autosave is written when you enter a new area, every three minutes of play, and when you leave or hide the browser page. Press F5, or tap the little floppy disk in the top left corner of the view, for a quick save.

The saves are listed under Menu, Save / Load (or Load game on the title screen). Each row shows the place, the faces and names of the party, the level, the round, the gold, how many heroes are alive, and the date.

SlotWhat it is
AutosaveWritten by the game. You can load or erase it, but not save over it.
Quick saveThe F5 key and the floppy disk. Also has a Save button.
Slot 1, 2, 3Yours. Each has Load, Save and Erase.
Previous runThe game you had before you began a new one. Load or erase only.
Older runThe one before that. Load or erase only.

You can save only when the round is over, and not in the middle of a talk or a cut scene. If you press Save too soon the game says “Wait for the round to finish, then save.” Erase asks twice: tap it, then tap “Sure? Tap again”.

Loading a game from inside a game first stores the one you were in as the Previous run, so a mistaken load can be undone. Import file takes a save file (.json) and puts it in Slot 3.

Where saves live: they are kept in your browser’s own storage, on the computer you are using. Another browser or another computer has its own, empty list, and clearing the browser’s site data clears the saves.

The portraits

The portrait with the yellow frame is the hero who will act next. A small badge at its foot gives the hero’s level. Beneath each portrait are four bars:

  • Red: health, with the numbers over it (34/34).
  • Blue: mana or stamina, the fuel for powers.
  • Thin yellow: experience towards the next level.
  • Thin brown: hunger. It turns orange when it gets low.

A portrait dimmed a little means that hero has acted this round. A red cross means a hero has fallen. Little squares at the top left of a portrait are afflictions that hero carries, and a row of them at the top right of the view shows what is afflicting the party (the initials beneath are the heroes it has struck). The Afflictions page, under the menu or on F, explains each one.

Rounds

Every living hero acts once in a round. When one acts, the yellow frame jumps to the next who has not. When all four have acted, creatures take their turn and the round counter in the place line goes up by one. To pass a round without acting, press Wait. Choose a different hero at any time by tapping the portrait (this opens the Items page) or by pressing 1 to 4 (this does not).

When no creature is near, walking does not wait for rounds at all: you simply go. When one is near, each step is a round, and the creatures move when you do. If you press a key while the party is busy, the game remembers it and does it as soon as it can.

Moving and Turning

The party stands in one square, facing north, east, south or west. A step moves you one square the way you face. A turn swings you a quarter of the way round. Walls, closed doors and large objects stop you with a bump; if a creature blocks the way the game says so.

Taking a step or a turn costs a minute of game time.

Keep going. Hold a movement key, or keep a finger on an arrow button, and the party keeps walking until you let go. On a game pad the left stick walks and steps sideways, the D-pad walks and turns, the bumpers turn, and the right stick turns.

People, Doors, Chests and Loot

Talking to people

Walk into a person, or tap them, to talk. If you tap from too far away the game says “Too far away. Step closer.” A speech box opens with their portrait and their words. Below it are your replies, each with a number. Tap one or press 1 to 4. Space or Enter hurries the writing along, and Esc ends the talk.

Look for the marks. A gold ! hangs over anyone who has a quest to give. A green ? hangs over anyone you can hand a finished quest in to. The same marks show on the minimap and on every area map, so you can find them from afar. When a quest is ready, the line A quest is ready. Press J. appears at the foot of the screen.

Doors

A wooden door opens when you walk into it (“You push the door open.”) and stays open. A locked door says so: “The door is locked. You need a key.” A rusty iron key picked up on the floor will open one; the number of keys you carry is shown at the foot of the screen and on the right. Other doors, in towns and ruins, are shut for other reasons, and the game tells you what the trouble is when you walk into them.

Picking things up

Things lying on the floor are yours for the taking. Step onto the square, or tap the thing when you are close: “You pick up the Old Dagger.” It goes into the party’s shared bag. If the bag is full the game says so and leaves it there. Keys go on the key ring instead of into the bag. A torch burning in a wall bracket can be taken too, by tapping it when you are close.

You can also drag a thing from the floor straight onto a hero’s portrait, or onto the Power or hand windows, to pick it up and put it in that hero’s hand at once.

To use what you have found, open the Items page (I). Tap an item, then tap a slot on the hero to equip it there, or press the Equip button, or E. Potions and food are used from the same place. Keys 1 to 4 choose which hero you are dressing.

Chests, barrels and the rest

Barrels, crates, chests, coffins, carts, vases and the like can be opened. Walk into one, or tap it when you are near. Any gold inside is yours as you open it (“You find 10 gold.”), and a window shows the rest. Your party is on the left, Found here on the right. Tap something to read it, then tap the button to take it, or press Take all. You can drag things across as well, and Sort tidies the bag. The same window serves for a merchant’s cart, and then the button buys and sells.

The Menu

Tap the Menu tab or press Esc. A tap anywhere outside the buttons, or Esc again, closes it. The menu is short, because the rest hides in two sub-menus, Settings and More.

Resume. Closes the menu.

Quests (key J). Your open and finished quests, by place. Tap a quest to circle where it is on the map.

Journal / Abilities (key L). A page of cards: see below.

Afflictions (key F). Every sickness, blessing and state, with how it feels, how you catch it and how to cure it.

Level layout (key M or O). The map of the place you are in.

Save / Load. Your slots.

Settings. Sound, Graphics, Gameplay, Controller and Keyboard.

More. Achievements, Stats, Collection, Wandering Monsters, New party and Restart game.

Exit game. Saves your Autosave and returns to the title screen.

Journal / Abilities

Each card opens a page. A small ! on a card means something new is waiting there. The pages are: The Story So Far; Reputation in each town; Rumours and Leads; Afflictions and States; Camp and Bonds; Skills and Trades; Crafting and Herbalism; Herbal Remedies; Companions; Weather and Seasons; and Luck and Omens.

Settings

Sound. Combat sounds, Ambient sounds, Music and Footsteps, each from 0 to 100 per cent in steps of ten.

Graphics. Dithering, Scanlines, Colour, Sharpening and Brightness, each with a few settings, and the CRT screen (glow and curved glass), On or Off. Colour is washed out by default, to look aged.

Gameplay. Switches between the Traditional and Scaled worlds and tells you the level of the place you are in.

Controller. A picture of the pad and what each button does.

Keyboard (key ? or F1). Every shortcut on one page.

More

Achievements. A list of every dungeon and every building, grouped by region, so you can see which you have visited.

Stats. Gold earned and spent, steps taken, days passed, places visited, quests, and a tally of monsters slain.

Collection (key U). Five logbooks to fill: Jewel Hunter, Butterfly Catching, Herb Collecting, Treasure and Shrine Logbook.

Wandering Monsters. Strange creatures that walk the levels without warning. The page is a list of question marks until you meet each one, and its About them button tells what is known of them.

New party. Back to choosing a party. The old run is kept as the Previous run if it was a real one.

Restart game. Saves an autosave and returns to the title screen.

The Items, Character, Spells and Powers pages

The row of four tabs along the foot of the Items page opens the pages of a hero. They have keys too: I Items (the bag), C Character sheet, B Spell book, K Powers. On any of them the number keys choose the hero. The Character sheet gives level, experience, health, stamina, strength, dexterity, intelligence, armour, damage, hit chance and gold. The Powers page lists each hero’s powers and has a Use button for each.

Maps and Quest Marks

The small window at the top right is the minimap: the squares you have seen, a gold arrow for the party, red dots for creatures near and in sight, and the quest marks. Squares you have not seen stay dark.

Watch your minimap for a ?

Hidden caves and wild dungeons lie off the roads, behind the trees. When your party looks towards one, a ? appears at the edge of the minimap and on the area maps. Follow the mark. It goes away once you have been inside.

Tap it, or press M or O, for the level layout: a parchment map of the whole place you are in. Drag the map to move it. The mouse wheel, or two fingers pinching, zooms it; the + and − buttons do the same, Fit shows the whole map and You centres it on the party. The legend marks quests, shops and doors, people, and gates or exits.

The World map button opens the map of the lands, with every region, its name and the levels of its places. It can be dragged and zoomed too, and Level map takes you back. Esc closes whichever you are on.

The marks, once more

A gold ! is a quest waiting to be given. A green ? is a quest ready to be handed in. They hang over the person in the 3D view, and the same marks are on the minimap and every area map. Never a gold question mark: gold is for new, green is for done.

Time, Hunger and Rest

Beneath the minimap runs the clock: Day 1 08:00, and under it the season and the weather. A new game starts at eight in the morning of day one, in spring. Each step, turn and round costs a minute. A night’s rest costs eight hours. There are four seasons, Spring, Summer, Autumn and Winter, each thirty days long. The sky follows the clock, so out of doors the light fades towards evening and the nights are dark.

Every hero has a hunger bar, the thin brown line under the portrait, from full (100) down to nothing. It sinks as time passes, and food fills it. A hero below a quarter is “hungry”, and at nothing is starving and loses health as the minutes go by. The Items page shows the exact figure for the chosen hero.

Press Camp (or R) to rest. You cannot rest with enemies close by. Camp opens a page of choices: resting the night heals everyone (a starving hero heals only to half) and restores mana and stamina. A fallen hero gets up again at half health. The same page offers cooking, mending the gear, tending the sick, tales round the fire, sparring and foraging. Full details of food, camp and bonds are in Chapter 2.

The First Ten Minutes

“You wake in the crypts below the abbey. Something is scratching in the dark...”

  1. Choose a party. If you are new, take the Ready-made party and the Traditional world, and press start.
  2. Look about. You are in the start crypt, and a few weapons and a potion lie on its floor. Step onto each to collect it.
  3. Dress the party. Press I. Choose a hero with 1 to 4, tap an item, then tap a slot to put it on. Do the same for each hero. Press I again to close.
  4. Open the door. Walk into the door to the east. Beyond it is the armoury, where a shield, a jerkin and a lantern wait to be picked up. Keep an eye on the minimap; it fills in as you go.
  5. Meet the rats. Sooner or later a rat comes into view. When a creature comes into view a plate shows its name, level and how hard a fight it will be. Press Attack for the hero in the yellow frame, then the next hero, and so on. Use Power when you want a stronger blow and have the stamina.
  6. Mind the health bars. Press Potion to heal the chosen hero. When the fight is over, Camp will mend everyone.
  7. Search. Open barrels and coffins, and pick up every key.
  8. Save. Press F5 before anything that frightens you.
  9. Climb out. Take the stairs up, and when you reach a town, talk to everyone. Follow the gold marks, and open the quest log with J.

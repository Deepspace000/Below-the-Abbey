Chapter 7Guide to the Lands: The Seas and the East

A traveller's guide to the Narrow Sea and everything under it, the long road to the far shore, and the grey castle city that waits at the end of the voyage.

When the party leaves Yarrim in the Dawn Walker, the map of the game changes. There are no roads. There is a sea of eight hundred by six hundred and forty squares, seventeen islands, four reefs, a monster the old sailors will not name, and a far shore where a king is waiting. This chapter is a guide to all of it, written in the manner of a good travel book: where to go, who you will meet, what they sell, what bites, and how hard the place is.

Every place in the game has a level, shown beside its name on the world map. The ratings in this chapter are those numbers. The Narrow Sea and its islands are fixed at about level 50, and Oldhaven and the lands behind it at 55. If your party is a good deal below those figures, go and gain strength elsewhere and return. Nothing at sea will wait for you to be ready, with one exception: the Kraken, which will let you turn back.

“Walk forward to sail.” The ship's own first words to you.

The guide is arranged in the order that a party meets the places. First the sea and how to sail it, then the four islands that matter to the story, the small islands nobody lives on, the reefs beneath, the Kraken, and last the Eastern Lands: the quay and the city of Oldhaven, the Oakwood behind it and the long Pilgrim's Path that runs home.

Sailing the Dawn Walker

The Dawn Walker was built by Menkaure the shipwright and lies at the end of the pier in Yarrim. Speak to Menkaure when your stores are aboard and the lamp is lit, and the ship casts off at evening with Lord Corvane's black ship hunting along the reef behind her. Your first landfall is Saltbarrow.

At the pier

Whenever the ship is tied up at a pier, speak to her and the chart is laid out on the table. It lists Saltbarrow always, the Isle of Bells once Maeve's work is done, Cinder Isle once Aldous is satisfied, and Last Light once Ketra is content. Choose a name and the Dawn Walker sails there on her own, and about six hours of the day go by on the water (eight if you sail back to Yarrim). The last choice on the chart is the important one: Take the helm and sail the open sea.

On the open sea

Here walking is sailing. You see the water over the bow rail, the rail rocks a little with the swell, and the arrow keys steer the ship exactly as they move the party on land. Every square you sail takes a few minutes of the day, so a long crossing feeds the party and moves the clock. The sea is full of things to look at: as many as sixteen ships under sail on the horizon, seven drifting wrecks, floating crates and barrels, and gulls over every island.

Press M for the map. At sea it opens zoomed right out, with every island named. The button at the lower right opens the world map, which shows the level of each place.

Landing buoys, Disembark and Embark

Every island has a landing buoy, a red and white float on a rope, moored on its south side. Walk into the buoy and you are offered a row ashore. Yarrim has a buoy on the west shore and Oldhaven has one on the far east coast.

You do not need a pier at all. Whenever the ship is within eight squares of an island's land, a Disembark button appears at the lower left of the view with the island's name above it. Press it and the party is put ashore on the beach nearest the ship, and the Dawn Walker rides at anchor just off the sand.

Ashore, whenever you are within eight squares of the ship, an Embark button appears in the same corner and takes you back aboard, exactly where she lay. If you walk up to the anchored ship and speak to her, you may climb aboard and sail from there as well. The button hides while a hostile creature is close by, so deal with the crabs first.

The rules of the sea, in brief

  • Walk forward to sail. The sea is open, and land is solid.
  • Within eight squares of an island, Disembark. Within eight squares of the ship, Embark.
  • Sea serpents hunt the open water west of the Kraken line. Keep the bow toward them.
  • At the pier, speak to the ship to read the chart or take the helm.
  • The Kraken does not leave its water. Sail back west and it sinks to wait for you.

The Narrow Sea

The Narrow Sea is not narrow. The west shore is Yarrim's coast, a long line of sand at the left of the map. The far east shore, across a hundred squares of cold flat water beyond the Kraken line, is the western edge of the Eastern Lands, where Oldhaven stands. Between them lie four named islands that carry the story, thirteen small ones that carry only treasure, and a great deal of water.

Sea serpents

About eighteen sea serpents hunt in the open water, away from the shores and the buoys. A Sea Serpent has 240 health before scaling and stands a level above your party. It hits for 12 to 19. The harbourmaster of Yarrim, Tamsin Kell, will pay a bounty on five of them.

Wrecks and flotsam

Seven wrecks drift on the open water and more lie on the islands. Walk up to one and look. Sable Maeve's lost captains kept logbooks, and some of the logs are still aboard.

The four islands

These four stand in a rough chain across the western sea, and the story leads from one to the next. Each has a pier with the Dawn Walker moored at the end, a landing buoy, and a dive line (see Diving).

Saltbarrow (level 49)

A pirate haven of sand, wrecks and canvas tents. Palms lean over the beach, crates are stacked beside the tents, a wreck lies on the sand and bones lie by the shore. The black ship Corvane sent after you has left forty marines camped along the north and east shore.

Who lives here. Sable Maeve, the pirate queen, who wants the marines gone and has news of the scribe Isolde to trade. Beside her camp stand a pirate cook and a deckhand, who talk freely.

Monsters. The marines are Bandits and Bandit Archers. The archers hide in the dunes.

Later. Maeve has more work for a party that finishes the first: the logbooks of her scattered fleet, and a flagship sunk on the reef below.

The Isle of Bells (level 49)

Mist over a drowned abbey. Three ruined arches hold three great green bronze bells, two to the north and one to the south. An altar stands in the middle, and standing stones watch the shore.

Who lives here. Brother Aldous, who sheltered the scribe three years ago and kept the first page she left. He will not give it to a stranger. Ring all three bells for him, and be ready for what wakes.

Monsters. Ghosts, Spectres and Skeletons. Each bell you ring raises more ghosts nearby, so do not ring them hurt.

Later. Aldous holds two further tasks: the silent bell below the waves, and the singing shell (see the Nautilus Helm, below).

Cinder Isle (level 49)

Black rock and red glow. The mountain breathes. The ground is bare black ash, with runestones, a tent or two, and six black crystals with cracks of red lying round the slopes.

Who lives here. Ketra, the ash-priestess, who keeps the second page and does not give things away. She wants three crystals and the Guardian that walks the high path.

Monsters. The Cinder Guardian, a thing the mountain made, with 260 health before scaling; also Skeletons and Giant Scorpions.

Last Light (level 49)

A scrap of rock at the end of the chart. A small lamp burns in a hut on the shore. Stones stand in a ring round an old altar at the top of the island, and a wreck lies on the beach.

Who lives here. Isolde, the exiled scribe, who wrote the master ledger in three copies and has lived in fear of the sea since. Be gentle with her.

Monsters. Assassins and Hitmen, the sort of people who are sent for a price.

Later. Isolde wants the great lens of the beacon raised from the wreck below, and it is she who knows where one of the Kraken guns lies.

The chain of three pages ends on the altar of Last Light, and Lord Corvane waits there. Do not climb to it with a wounded party. Chapter 8 has more.

The thirteen small islands

Nobody lives on the other islands, and each is built the moment you reach it. All are level 50. On every island you will find chests (salt has eaten the locks): gold, a potion or a draught, and a little experience. Wrecks, altars and stone rings are scattered about, and gulls stand on the shore. A potion lies near the landing point of each.

The islands come in kinds. A tiny island is a speck of sand with a camp or a chest. A rock is bare black or red stone, with wrecks and arches. A forest island is big, green and full of wolves.

Diving: the underwater dungeons

Below each of the four main islands lies a reef, a great seabed one hundred and ten squares wide: a sunlit sea for a sky, fish and schools of fish drifting overhead, coral and kelp standing where trees would stand, and a bubbling vent here and there that gives air. These are dungeons in all but the name, and the creatures in them bite.

The Diving Helm

Every hero must wear a Diving Helm in the helmet slot before the dive line will take you down. Tam Salvor, the helm-maker in Yarrim, sells them (300 gold each, armour 3), in his own brass suit so that he is easy to find. A helm gives four minutes of air at a time.

Air

The air bar sits at the top of the view. You will be warned at one minute, at thirty seconds and at ten. When the air is gone the party starts drowning, losing a few per cent of their health every two seconds, and after fourteen seconds a dive line hauls them up to the surface. A vent is a stream of bubbles in the seabed: stand within one square of it and the party breathes freely until the air is full again. Each reef has four.

A Potion of the Gill gives the whole party the dive for good and removes the need for helms. It is priced at 2500 gold. A bottle lies in the great chest under Last Light, and a chapel in Yarrim keeps another for those who bring it pearls.

Dive from the south beach of an island: a red and white buoy on a rope, the dive line, runs down into the water. Underwater, the line is the buoy near where you arrive, and it takes you up again.

What lives and what you find

Reef Sharks, Barracuda, Reef Crabs, Octopuses, Glow Jellies and Moray Eels live in every reef, and Spiny Puffers in the last two. Some of the fish overhead are only scenery, and do not bite. All four reefs are level 50.

Up to ten giant clams lie on each reef floor. About half hold a white pearl, and the rest hold a few coins. Up to eight shell-clasped chests hold gold and a potion or a draught. The beast of each reef guards a great chest at the end of its arena, and the prize belongs to the dive quest set by the islander on the surface: Maeve's sunk fortune, the bell's clapper, the Heart of the Vent and the lens of the beacon.

Hint: Once Pell, the chandler in Yarrim, has asked for wreck-oak, cut the planks of black oak you pass in the reefs. He turns three of them into a harpoon.

The Nautilus Helm

After the Isle of Bells quest is finished, Brother Aldous tells you of a great spiral shell that broke in four in a storm, one piece sinking beneath each island: the Whorl under Saltbarrow, the Lip under the Isle of Bells, the Spire under Cinder Isle and the Pearl-Lining under Last Light.

A piece lies in a far corner of every reef, in a pearl-white clam that glows. It is not like the clams that hold pearls, and it stays shut until Aldous has asked you to find it. Bring all four back to him and the shell mends itself into the Nautilus Helm: armour 9, it restores the wearer's health every round of a battle, brings mana back faster, and lets them breathe under the sea.

Hint: Leave the paths. The glowing clam sits in a corner, well away from the rest.

The Kraken

The Kraken stands between the two coasts of the Narrow Sea. It is twenty levels above your party, with a thousand suckers, and nothing your party carries can hurt it. No ship has crossed the deep water in a hundred years.

The Kraken waters

There is a line far to the east, about five hundred and forty-five squares across the map. As you approach it the message comes: the water ahead is flat and cold. No gulls fly east of here. Cross it and the sea turns black. Far ahead, seventeen or more squares, something vast rises out of the water, and it comes for you.

You may run. If you sail back west, and the Kraken is more than fourteen squares behind, it sinks into the dark and waits. Once it is slain the sea to the east is open for good.

The Kraken guns

The old fleet of the Crown killed the Kraken once, with four magical harpoons forged for nothing else. When the fleet burned the guns were scattered over the sea. When the ledger is complete, Menkaure lays it on the chart table and tells you where to look: ask Ketra, Sable Maeve, Brother Aldous and Isolde. Each knows where one lies.

Every hero needs one in hand. Each does between 3000 and 4400 damage to the Kraken, and next to nothing to anything else. When the Kraken is dead the four flare white and fall to ash, because they were never meant to outlast it.

The four guns lie on the Redcliff altar (the Ember Harpoon, from Ketra), in the flagship wrecked on Kelpgate Rock (the Verdigris Harpoon, from Maeve), among the standing stones of the Hollow Isle (the Silver Harpoon, from Aldous) and in the wreck beneath Last Light (the Beacon Harpoon, from Isolde, which needs a dive). Menkaure sets the task once you hold the third page of the ledger.

Hint: Equip a harpoon in every hero before you cross the line. A hero with a sword in hand will do nothing at all to the Kraken.

The Eastern Lands

With the Kraken dead the harbour boom of Oldhaven is down, and a long grey sea wall runs along the shore, with the towers of the king's city rising over it. The Eastern Lands are the country of King Osric: a quay, a castle city, an oak wood behind it and a pilgrim's road that leads home.

Oldhaven Quay (level 55)

The king's harbour. The Dawn Walker ties up at the end of a long pier. Five piers run out into the water with big ships moored between them, small boats rock in the swell, and flags in the blue and white of Oldhaven fly from every pole. Under the grey wall, a paved plaza fills with fishwives, sailors, stevedores and soldiers in squads of two or three. Dogs trot about the stalls.

The great gate stands at the north end of the plaza in the line of the wall: three squares of portcullis between two wings of dressed stone with arrow slits, and a round tower at each end. The keep and spires of the castle loom above it. Gargoyles crouch along the top of the gatehouse, and four more on plinths stand sentinel at the foot. On your first visit the gate is barred until noon, and the harbour herald will tell you so.

The fish market

On the west of the plaza is the fish market, four stalls each with its own catch. Marta, the fishwife, sells herring, mackerel, sardines, crab and oysters, and bread. Big Hobb, the fishmonger, has haddock, salt cod and jellied eel, with oysters and ale. Pol, the quay cook, serves fried fish in a cone and bowls of chowder. Warrick, in the Fishmonger's Hall, has the lot.

The fish are the cheapest food in the east, and heal as well as feed. A bowl of chowder heals 36 and feeds 100.

The people of the quay talk, and it is worth walking among them. A harbourmaster counts every ship that comes in, an old fisherman warns you not to fish off the end of the east pier (“something big takes the bait”), a ship's captain asks about the gull on your prow, and two guards at the gate swear they have not seen four people who look like you.

Oldhaven, the castle city (level 55)

Beyond the portcullis lies a grey stone castle city, one hundred and fifty squares by one hundred and fifty, ringed by a curtain wall with a tower at every corner. The king's road runs straight north from the gate to a great square before the castle, lined with soldiers, and the streets run east and west of it about three houses apart, with lanterns, flowers and a door on every house. There are no monsters in the streets. Nothing is hostile here, and it is a good place to rest, shop and heal.

The districts

The city map names its quarters. At the south, the Portcullis Gate and the King's Road run north to the Great Square and the Castle Ward, with the Castle of King Osric beyond. In the west lie Old Town and Chapel Close, with Lantern Street in the north-west and the Garrison Quarter in the south-west. In the east are the Market Square, the Fair Green, the East Road and the Smiths Quarter. The Land Gate is in the north wall, at the top of the street east of the castle.

There are three other gates. The great Portcullis Gate in the south leads to the quay. The Land Gate in the north wall leads to the Pilgrim's Path. The East Gate at the end of the East Road is shut at first: it is held by Chancellor Vance's Ash Guard until the city's siege is won, and the gate-captain can tell you more.

The court of King Osric

The castle holds the court, a long hall of grey stone with two thrones at the far end, banners in the royal blue and white, statues, braziers and a household of courtiers, soldiers and clerks. King Osric is an old man who once knew the names of his captains. Captain Aldwyn of the Gate will receive petitioners. At noon on the day you arrive, you are brought before the king, and what you carry will decide how the day goes.

The court is where the master ledger is read and the warrant against your party is torn up. The names in the margin of the ledger lead to the Fair of Saint Brannoch, the siege and the Oakwood. Chapter 8 follows these.

The Oakwood (level 55)

When the East Gate is open it leads into the Oakwood, an old oak forest with six glades joined by paths: the Gate Glade, the Woodwarden Camp, the Oakwrights' Yard, Bramwick's Glade, the Ashen Glade and the Great Oak.

Who lives here. Hale, captain of the woodwardens, with his woodwardens and the young Wren; Master Joss and his Oakwrights, who plant acorns and wait a hundred years; Bramwick, the hedge-wizard; and Ysolt, keeper of the Great Oak.

Monsters. Grey Wolves, and the Ash Guard, who keep a camp in the Ashen Glade.

The Pilgrim's Path (level 55)

For the overland journey home, take the Land Gate in the north wall of Oldhaven. A path begins there and runs north up the peninsula, with the sea on the left hand and an old forest on the right, then west over the mountains and south again, until it ends at a high cliff above Yarrim. It is ninety-six kilometres long, with a signpost at every kilometre, and it goes one way only. The cliff at the end can only be climbed down.

There is no wagon on this road, and no stage. The only way along it is on foot, and it is built to be walked slowly: flowers, lamps, a few houses, inns and chapels, and a painted pilgrims' shrine on the left every two kilometres with food to take.

On the way

Thirteen buildings stand along the path, including three inns (the Last Lamp, the Cold Spring Inn and the Wayfarer's Rest) and three chapels (the Church of the Long Road, the Chapel of the Quiet and Saint Aldric's by the Sea). Thick woods run along the sea, with deer, rabbits, squirrels, bees, bears, slimes and wolves. A moss-covered stone cross beside the road marks every hidden cave and wild dungeon, and a bard, Tamsin Larkwhistle, sings in the woods each evening.

The twenty stones

A stone teleporter stands beside every fifth signpost, and another by the cliff above Yarrim: twenty in all. Wake them every one and an achievement comes up, with 1000 gold and the four pieces of the Abbot's armour, a mitre, a plate chasuble, cuffs and greaves with a set bonus that any class can wear.

Saint Elen's Monastery

Halfway along the Path, a gate cut into the rock of the pass leads into green grounds: a church, a cloister with a fountain, a refectory, a scriptorium, a kitchen, a guest house, an infirmary, gardens, an orchard, hives and a bell. The monks are friendly. East of the grounds lies a quiet cemetery with a mausoleum, and below them a cellar and old crypts that have a ghost problem. A lane from the Path to the gate begins at the foot of a great mossy statue of Saint Elen.

Hint: Carry a lantern for the mountain nights, and do not turn back at the cliff. Sister Margery in Oldhaven will bless you before you set out.

A traveller's checklist

  • A Diving Helm for every hero, or the Potion of the Gill.
  • A weapon in hand for every hero, and a full pack of food and potions before the Kraken line.
  • A lantern for the mountain nights on the Pilgrim's Path.
  • Gold for the fish stalls, the inns and the armourers of Oldhaven.
  • Patience: the sea is large, and a day goes by quickly on the water.

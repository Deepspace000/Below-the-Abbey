Below the Abbey: update 3.514 (changes since 3.482, the update on itch.io this morning)

The Hollow Grasslands: language and the four cities

* All the dialogue in the grasslands is gibberish until the party has found the four books from the wizard. Hollow words now follow the style of the city names.
* A fixed hollow vocabulary: common words such as I, city, house and work always get the same hollow word. 536 words in all, found by reading every line of dialogue in the grasslands.
* A word you meet twice can be asked about. Ask the folk, and the word is solved, added to the new Grassland Tongue glossary in the menu, and always understood from then on.
* When you learn a word, it now shows at once in the message that taught it. You can only learn words from what people say, and buying an item teaches you its name.
* The folk now talk at length. Vendors tell you about their wares, and philosophers walk the streets saying interesting things. The long speeches make the books worth finding.
* Market stalls in the four cities now sell fruit and vegetables (six ranges across the markets). More townsfolk shop and tend the stalls.
* Each city has a pet shop. The sellers have eighteen little companions from the grasslands, and small versions of the giants. Up to three follow the party, and each gives a small gift: more armour, a better chance to hit, gold from fights, a bigger bag, or a little healing each round.
* Townsfolk and small creatures walk more slowly with a gentler bounce, and a seated person no longer turns to face a new way.
* The big creatures of Exsce are kept apart, in a large middle section where they live. The peacock no longer vanishes when you walk near it, and the giants no longer flash. Each city has two mazes instead of four.
* The Bell Tower of Lanterns has a chest at the top and a teleport stone at the top and at the base.
* The Hollow Grasslands map shows the painted frog-whale picture. The Spindle Tyrant's stray claw is gone, and the stilt-walker ostrich has its face on the front of its head. Planets lift over buildings instead of passing through them.

The deeper Hollow Lands

* The Hollow Child-King's corruption is seeping up into the grasslands from a place further down. Once the four books are in, Aldous Wend tells the party about it, and a chain of three main quests begins.
* Two great flying creatures can find the way: Old Lilac, the great moth, in Glorm, and Bulb, the great whale, in Nibb. The party finds the moth, brings her six jars of royal jelly, then clears eight creatures of the hives for the whale. Then Bulb carries the party down through the old gate.
* The deeper Hollow Lands are one giant maze, 200 by 200 squares, of winding one-square corridors. It starts as a hedge maze and climbs through stone, honeycomb, shell, sandstone and three coloured quarters. Rooms, round clearings and large atriums full of trees, plants and ponds open up inside it. Trees only grow in the open places, never in a narrow passage. Hollowed creatures roam it, and the Hollowed Warden waits in the great atrium at the top. On the maze maps the names are a separate layer: small, without halos or boxes, so they never hide the corridors, and they get smaller (and the precinct names fade) as you zoom in. A medieval map of the whole maze and a local map of each of its four quarters, with every corridor on them, and a small copy of the maze on the world map.
* Eight safe towns, one in each quarter of the maze: Hedgefoot, Cairnhold, Combwick, Nacre Landing, Dunewell, Blue Lantern, Tessera and Gildmere, and four lone enclaves in rooms and clearings. Twelve quest givers and eight shops live in them. The people stay in the safe places and often warn the party to keep out of the danger zones.
* Ten enclaves (safe rooms and clearings) are spread through the maze as places to rest. Fourteen danger zones, each with its own name and signs at the corridors that lead in, are full of hollow slugs, hollow jellies, hollow worms, big bugs, cave spiders and lantern beetles. The rest of the maze is peaceful.
* Secret doors everywhere: a wall of a slightly different shade, which opens when you walk into it. Eighty of them, half are shortcuts across the maze, half hide treasure chambers at the end of dead-end corridors, each with a chest.
* The three kinds of door now look different. A secret door is the wall with a faint arched seam and a few scuffs. A key door is a banded wooden door with an iron lock plate and a stripe in the key's colour. A gem door is a stone slab with a gold ring and a large glowing gem of the colour it needs.
* Fifty-eight hidden chests in all. Twelve doors need a coloured key (red, blue, green or gold), and six need a gem (ruby, sapphire, emerald, amber, amethyst or pearl). Keys and gems are used up in the lock. They turn up in vault chests, as quest rewards, lying in the danger zones, and in the shops.
* Every danger zone has a named champion with a guarded chest beside it, such as Old Gnaw the Great Slug, Mother Silk and the Lantern King. Each chest holds a key or a gem, plus treasure.
* Forty-four notes are scratched and chalked on the walls of the maze: jokes, lore and hints. Statues and plinths stand in the atriums, and the towns have lamps and three more villagers each.
* The Underlevel: twelve ladders in the clearings of the maze go down to a very dark, sparse labyrinth of long straight corridors under it. Walk into a ladder and you are asked if you want to descend, with a warning to bring food and torches. The same twelve ladders lead back up, so a long walk under the ground comes up in a different precinct. There is no food, no torch and no gold down there, only a great many tough dark insects that give little xp and leave afflictions, and the Messenger of Death wanders there with his five letters.
* A teleport stone stands in every town and every safe enclave of the maze, and a few stand at the far ends of the danger zones so a party that has gone too far can get back. They are the usual teleport stones: wake one by standing on it, press T, and the world map lets you travel to any woken stone anywhere in the game. Tap the Deeper Hollow on the world map to choose which of its woken stones to go to. There are none in the Underlevel.
* The map screen of the maze has an Under button that shows the Underlevel: its dark corridors, its ladders and your footsteps there.
* Every outdoor area now has a butterfly meadow: butterflies live in one patch of about a tenth of the ground, joined to the rest of the map so you can walk to it. The maze has a meadow in each of its four quarters. You still need a butterfly cage.
* Buried treasure lies in about one square in a hundred of every map. Dig with a shovel to find a strongbox with gold, and often a gem and a potion. A Ranger, Thief or Assassin in the party senses it nearby and says "There is treasure buried around here somewhere."
* More life in the maze: eight thinkers walk slowly up and down the safe passages and say wise things about mazes, little creatures of the grassland wander the open places, and herbs lie on the ground here and there. There are 48 statues at dead ends and 70 cracked walls that crumble when you walk into them, a way through to the other side of the passage.
* The Drowned Maze: four flights of steps in the pond clearings of the maze lead down into a flooded part of the labyrinth. It is a true underwater level: the party needs a Diving Helm or a Bubble Helm (a hero without one drowns), has four minutes of air, and breathes at bubble vents. Sea creatures swim its passages, shell chests hold pearls and gems, and a warden sleeps at the far end. The prompt warns you about helms before you go down.
* Two boat canals: wooden docks in four fairly empty parts of the maze let the party hop on a boat and steer it through a maze of water passages, the walls hung thick with vines, in the Vine Canal and the Croc Canals. Swamp crocodiles, marsh eels and vine snakes swim there. At the far dock the party lands in another part of the maze and goes on on foot.
* Hidden footplate traps in some passages spring spikes, darts, poison gas or a rockfall. A Thief, Assassin or Ranger in the party spots a plate three squares ahead and warns the party. Tap the floor or press Attack to set it off safely from a distance.
* Quests of the water: Old Gill, a retired diver in Nacre Landing, sets four tasks in the Drowned Maze (pearls, the warden Old Gulper, the bubble vents, the shell chests). Reed, the canal warden in Blue Lantern, sets five in the water passages (crocodiles, eels, snakes, the far docks, herbs for an antidote). Each gives the next when you finish the last.
* New painted art: the huts, tents, ladders, steps, docks, doors, warning signs and teleport stones on the maze maps, and the walls of the secret, cracked, key, gem, ladder, steps, dock and vine walls, are now painted pictures instead of shapes drawn by the game.
* Ornate symbols drawn straight onto the walls of the maze, like tattoos or cave paintings, each biome with its own sign (a thorn knot, a cairn and hammer, a honeycomb and bee, a nautilus, a sun and scarab, a lantern, a mosaic eye, a crown and sunburst) and a few common ones (a coiled snake, a moon, a tree, a hand with an eye). Round gilded shields hang in the built-up places, the towns and the stone rooms. All of them are painted pictures.
* Grass and small wild plants (tall grass, tufts, ferns, glowing blue mushrooms, bluebells, thistles, moss, lilies) grow out of the ground in eight wilder parts of the maze, and four kinds of vine hang on the walls. Clouds drift slowly through seven sections of the maze, only ever over open floor and never through a wall.
* Four thieves wander the labyrinth, one in each quarter, outside the towns. They show as red marks on the mini map, and creep up behind the party when it is near. If the party does not turn to face a thief in time he takes up to 500 gold and runs off. If the party turns to face him, he is caught and the fight begins; kill him and the stolen gold comes back. A Thief, Ranger or Assassin in the party says "A thief is near, be on guard."
* Each thief lives in a lone house in a clearing of his quarter, and is at home whenever the party comes near it. Accuse him and he tries to talk the party out of it with three excuses; if they keep to the accusation each time, he gives back all the gold he took from them. If they waver, he sends them away and they can try again. He can also be fought. If the party has killed a thief, his house is empty and a chest there holds some of his gold.
* Fixed: the little pet creatures and the drifting clouds in the maze were not moving.
* Bulb gives the party a magic map of silver thread when the whale's errand is done. From then on, the map of the maze draws a thin, glowing, dashed line of everywhere the party has walked. The party is told of it when it first enters the maze.
* The towns on the maze maps are now small huts in the style of their biome (thatch, stone, honeycomb skep, shell roof, sandstone dome, tent, mosaic pavilion, gilded dome), small enough to leave the maze showing. Ladders are marked too.
* The people speak the Hollow tongue plus a deep dialect of maze words (maze, hedge, key, gem, door, secret, danger and more). Ask about a word you have heard twice to learn it, and it goes into the Grassland Tongue glossary.

The Orange Spire

* The spire was rebuilt. It has 1 room on the first floor, one more on each floor up to 10 on the tenth, then back down to 1 on the nineteenth. The twentieth floor has 2 rooms with a small labyrinth between them. More tall pointed windows with a dark view outside.
* Floor 20 is a deathtrap labyrinth. Hidden plates in the floor send slow fireballs down the passages, spike tiles wait in the corridors, and figures jump out in the dark. Little alcoves in the side walls give you a place to step out of the way.
* The fireballs fly at half the old speed, and the plates and spike tiles are armed again every time you enter the floor.
* Before you enter the labyrinth the heroes talk about the death trap. One of them has read a book about a place like it, and the others know the book.
* The thief has a new power, Sense Traps, which shows the traps nearby.
* The Messenger of Death now waits on floor 19 only, not 17. His letters are hidden in... well, that would be telling, wouldn't it.
* The Orange Spire vampire no longer heals itself.
* The plaque at the foot of the Violet Tower now has a portal to the Hollow Grasslands instead of a dead end.
* Floor 19 is now in the Go to area list on the title screen.

Fighting, healing and difficulty

* Hero health grows with level as fast as enemy health does, so a level 70 hero is as tough as a level 70 place expects. Older saves are brought up to the new rule when they load.
* Heals now restore a share of the patient's own health: 28 percent plus 0.9 percent per power level for one patient, and 12 percent plus 0.6 percent per level for a party. The listed amount is the minimum.
* Healing powers now cost about 30 percent more mana, so a healer runs dry in a long fight and needs a mana potion.
* The potion message shows the health actually gained.
* A new difficulty selector in the Gameplay menu. It moves in steps of 10 percent, from 80 percent easier to 200 percent harder. Enemies hit harder, take a little more to kill, hit more often and are hit less often. Gold and XP move with it.
* The difficulty page shows a worked example for a real creature nearby: its damage, hit chances, hits to kill, and the XP and gold a kill gives, now against Normal.
* The fight rating can read Hard for a creature below the party's level.

Controls and the screen

* Right-drag looks left and right as well as up and down, further and faster. Look up and down works everywhere: right-drag, V for mouse look, the right stick, two fingers on a touch screen and a look slider. Left and right are inverted by default, with a switch in the Gameplay menu.
* Hover your mouse over a button or icon to see its shortcut key.
* The controller pointer and its gold frame are drawn in front of the stone buttons.
* The butterfly catch button is at the bottom right of the screen, is off during battle, and there are 40 percent fewer butterflies.
* The quest log lists the newest quest first, with a box of the main quests at the top.
* The inventory torch looks like a normal hand torch. The big A in the title logo is larger and sits a little lower and to the right.

Fixes

* The cutscene of leaving the abbey now plays once, not after every wild dungeon.
* Herbs picked from the ground now count for every quest that asks for herbs, so the Cave Witch no longer says you have none.
* The Oldhaven quay opens to the sea on the west. Bubble helms cost 800 gold, and a party can dive with fewer than four helms, but a hero without one drowns.

Books

* Fifteen books lie in rooms, tower tops and hidden corners of the four cities. Pick one up and read it page by page, from 2 to 20 pages. Ten of them raise one stat of each hero by 1, once for good. Five are plain tales. Books can be sold: the ones that raise a stat fetch 400 to 800 gold.
